Exercises: 4. Continuous time

All waveforms and coordinates are real. Energies and inner products are over the whole real line. A pulse is zero outside its stated support. Coordinate vectors use the ordered orthonormal basis specified in the question. For white Gaussian noise, $N_0/2$ is the two-sided power spectral density, with $N_0>0$, and projections onto orthonormal directions have independent zero-mean Gaussian noise of variance $N_0/2$. The noise is independent of the message. For $M$ equiprobable messages, $E_{\rm b}$ is average message energy divided by $\log_2 M$. ML is minimum-distance decoding in these coordinates; it is optimal for equiprobable messages. With unequal priors use MAP where requested. Probability-zero ties may be assigned arbitrarily. Exact expressions in terms of $\operatorname{erfc}$ are sufficient. Distinguish squared distance from distance, and energy per coordinate from energy per information bit.

Exercise 4.1.

A real waveform is $x(t)=2$ on $[0,3)$ and zero elsewhere. What is its energy?

(a) $4$
(b) $6$
(c) $12$

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

Section 4.1 defines energy as the integral of the squared waveform. Here $E=\int_0^3 2^2\,\mathrm dt=12$. Integrating the amplitude would give 6, which is not energy.

Exercise 4.2.

A real waveform has energy $9$. What is its norm?

(a) $3$
(b) $9$
(c) $81$

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

By Section 4.1, the norm is the square root of the energy, so $\|x\|=\sqrt9=3$. Energy is the squared norm, not the norm itself.

Exercise 4.3.

Let $x(t)=2$ on $[0,2)$ and $y(t)=-1$ on $[1,3)$, both zero elsewhere. What is $\langle x,y\rangle$?

(a) $0$
(b) $-4$
(c) $-2$

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

The product is nonzero only on the overlap $[1,2)$, where it equals $-2$. Thus $\langle x,y\rangle=\int_1^2(-2)\,\mathrm dt=-2$. Nonzero overlap does not imply a positive inner product.

Exercise 4.4.

Real signals have energies $\|x\|^2=4$ and $\|y\|^2=9$, with $\langle x,y\rangle=-3$. What is the energy of $x+y$?

(a) $10$
(b) $7$
(c) $13$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

The sum identity of Section 4.1 gives $\|x+y\|^2=4+9+2(-3)=7$. Adding energies alone is valid when the inner product vanishes. The negative cross term lowers the energy here.

Exercise 4.5.

Let $u(t)=1$ on $[0,1)$ and let $v(t)$ equal $1$ on $[0,\tfrac12)$ and $-1$ on $[\tfrac12,1)$, all zero elsewhere. Which statement is true?

(a) They cannot be orthogonal because their supports overlap.
(b) They are orthonormal despite sharing the same support.
(c) They are orthogonal but have different energies.

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

Both squared waveforms integrate to 1. Their inner product is $\tfrac12-\tfrac12=0$, so they are orthonormal. Section 4.1 permits overlapping orthogonal signals because positive and negative product contributions can cancel.

Exercise 4.6.

Let $x(t)=3$ on $[0,2)$, zero elsewhere. Which multiple of $x$ has unit energy?

(a) $x/18$
(b) $x/(3\sqrt2)$
(c) $x/3$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

The energy of $x$ is $9\cdot2=18$. Multiplication by $c$ scales energy by $c^2$, so normalization divides by $\sqrt{18}=3\sqrt2$. Dividing by energy rather than its square root would not give unit energy.

Exercise 4.7.

A real signal $x$ has energy $6$. What is the energy of $y(t)=x(2t)$?

(a) $6$
(b) $3$
(c) $12$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

Changing variable $u=2t$ in the energy integral gives $E_y=\tfrac12\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|x(u)|^2\,\mathrm du=3$. Time compression by two halves the duration while keeping the amplitude scale.

Exercise 4.8.

A finite-energy signal $x$ is replaced by $y(t)=x(t-5)$. How does its energy change?

(a) It is unchanged.
(b) It is multiplied by $5$.
(c) It decreases because the signal starts later.

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

A time shift changes the location of the waveform, not the integral of its square over the whole real line. Substitution $u=t-5$ leaves $E_y=E_x$. This does not assert that energy in a fixed observation window is unchanged.

Exercise 4.9.

Let $x(t)=2t$ for $0\leq t<1$, zero elsewhere. What is its energy?

(a) $\tfrac43$
(b) $2$
(c) $1$

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

The energy integral is $\int_0^1 4t^2\,\mathrm dt=4/3$. The integral of $2t$ is 1, but energy requires squaring before integrating. This also distinguishes a ramp from a constant pulse of the same peak amplitude.

Exercise 4.10.

A real waveform has energy zero. What follows?

(a) It may be positive on half of an interval and negative on the other half, provided the two areas cancel.
(b) It must be zero at every single time.
(c) It is zero almost everywhere.

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

The nonnegative function $|x(t)|^2$ has zero integral, hence vanishes almost everywhere. Here, 'almost everywhere' allows exceptional sets of times with zero measure, such as isolated instants. Their values do not affect energy. Cancellation of positive and negative amplitudes concerns the integral of $x$, not of its square.

Exercise 4.11.

On $[0,1)$ let $\phi_1(t)=1$, and let $\phi_2(t)=1$ on the first half and $-1$ on the second. All signals vanish elsewhere. A waveform has levels $3$ and $-1$ on these halves. What are its coordinates in $(\phi_1(t),\phi_2(t))$?

(a) $(2,1)$
(b) $(3,-1)$
(c) $(1,2)$

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

The two pulses are orthonormal. Section 4.2 gives coordinates by inner products, so $x_1=(3-1)/2=1$ and $x_2=(3+1)/2=2$. Reconstructing $\phi_1(t)+2\phi_2(t)$ gives levels 3 and -1.

Exercise 4.12.

Let $\phi_1(t)=1$ on $[0,1)$ and $\phi_2(t)$ have levels $1,-1$ on its two halves, zero elsewhere. What are the two levels of $x(t)=2\phi_1(t)-3\phi_2(t)$?

(a) $2$ and $-3$
(b) $-1$ and $5$
(c) $5$ and $-1$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

On the first half, both basis pulses equal 1, giving $2-3=-1$. On the second, $\phi_2(t)=-1$, giving $2+3=5$. Coordinates specify weights of the chosen basis, not necessarily time-domain levels.

Exercise 4.13.

In an orthonormal basis, a real signal has coordinate vector $(1,-2,2)$. What is its energy?

(a) $9$
(b) $5$
(c) $1$

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

By the coordinate energy identity of Section 4.2, the energy is $1^2+(-2)^2+2^2=9$. Signed coordinates do not subtract energy. The sum of absolute coordinates, 5, is also not the squared Euclidean norm.

Exercise 4.14.

Two signals have coordinates $(1,2)$ and $(3,-1)$ in the same orthonormal basis. What is their squared distance?

(a) $13$
(b) $5$
(c) $\sqrt{13}$

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

The difference vector is $(-2,3)$, whose energy is $4+9=13$. Section 4.2 preserves waveform distances under orthonormal coordinates. The distance itself is $\sqrt{13}$, but the question asks its square.

Exercise 4.15.

Let $x(t)=3\phi_1(t)+\phi_2(t)$ for an orthonormal pair. A new basis is $\psi_1(t)=(\phi_1(t)+\phi_2(t))/\sqrt2$, $\psi_2(t)=(\phi_1(t)-\phi_2(t))/\sqrt2$. What are the new coordinates?

(a) $(3,1)$
(b) $(4,2)$
(c) $(2\sqrt2,\sqrt2)$

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

The new pulses are also orthonormal. Their inner products with $x$ give $(3+1)/\sqrt2=2\sqrt2$ and $(3-1)/\sqrt2=\sqrt2$. Their squared coordinates add to $8+2=10$, agreeing with $3^2+1^2$.

Exercise 4.16.

For an orthonormal pair, let $x(t)=2\phi_1(t)+3\phi_2(t)$. If only its projection onto the span of $\phi_1(t)$ is retained, what is the error energy?

(a) $13$
(b) $9$
(c) $4$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

The retained part is $2\phi_1(t)$ and the residual is $3\phi_2(t)$. The latter has energy 9 and is orthogonal to the retained part. The identity $13=4+9$ checks the projection decomposition.

Exercise 4.17.

A nonzero real signal $u$ has energy $4$, and $\langle x,u\rangle=6$. What is the projection of $x$ onto the line spanned by $u$?

(a) $\tfrac32u$
(b) $3u$
(c) $6u$

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

For a non-unit direction the coefficient is $\langle x,u\rangle/\|u\|^2=6/4=3/2$. Normalizing $u$ first gives the same result. The coefficient 6 would incorrectly treat $u$ as a unit-energy direction.

Exercise 4.18.

Let $x(t)=t$ on $[0,1)$, zero elsewhere, and let $\phi(t)=1$ on that interval. What is the energy of the residual after projecting $x(t)$ onto $\phi(t)$?

(a) $\tfrac13$
(b) $\tfrac14$
(c) $\tfrac1{12}$

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

The unit pulse gives coordinate $\int_0^1t\,\mathrm dt=1/2$, so the projection has energy $1/4$. The original energy is $1/3$. Orthogonality of the residual gives $1/3-1/4=1/12$, also obtained by integrating $(t-1/2)^2$.

Exercise 4.19.

Let $x(t)=2$ on $[0,\tfrac12)$ and zero elsewhere. Project onto the unit pulse $\phi(t)=1$ on $[0,1)$. Which pair gives the projection energy and residual energy?

(a) $(2,0)$
(b) $(1,2)$
(c) $(1,1)$

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

The coordinate is $\langle x(t),\phi(t)\rangle=1$, so the projection is the level-1 rectangle over the whole unit interval and has energy 1. The original energy is $4/2=2$, leaving residual energy 1. The residual has levels 1 and -1 on the two halves.

Exercise 4.20.

Let $\bar x$ be the orthogonal projection of $x$ onto a finite-dimensional signal space $V$. When does $\|\bar x\|^2=\|x\|^2$ hold?

(a) For every $x$, because projection always preserves energy.
(b) Exactly when $x$ belongs to $V$, up to equality almost everywhere.
(c) Only when $x$ is the zero signal.

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

Section 4.2 gives $\|x\|^2=\|\bar x\|^2+\|x-\bar x\|^2$. Equality of the first two terms holds precisely when the residual has zero energy. A nonzero signal already in $V$ is recovered exactly.

Exercise 4.21.

Two transmitted signals have coordinates $(1,1)$ and $(-1,-1)$ in a two-dimensional orthonormal basis. What is the dimension of their span?

(a) $1$
(b) It depends on the chosen orthonormal basis.
(c) $2$

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

The second signal is the negative of the first, so both lie on one line. Two available coordinate directions do not imply that the transmitted signals span two dimensions. Their common line can be represented by one unit-energy waveform. A change of orthonormal basis does not change the dimension of this span.

Exercise 4.22.

Two signals have coordinates $(1,1)$ and $(1,-1)$ in an orthonormal basis. Which description is correct?

(a) They are identical except for a change of basis.
(b) They are antipodal and span one dimension.
(c) They are orthogonal and span two dimensions.

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

Their inner product is $1-1=0$, and both have nonzero energy 2. Two nonzero orthogonal vectors are linearly independent, so the span has dimension 2. Antipodal vectors would have all coordinates reversed.

Exercise 4.23.

Four equiprobable messages have signal energies $0,2,4,6$. Each message carries two information bits. What is the average energy per information bit?

(a) $3$
(b) $\tfrac32$
(c) $6$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

The average message energy is $(0+2+4+6)/4=3$. Section 4.3 divides this by the two information bits per message, giving $E_{\rm b}=3/2$. The zero-energy message still has probability 1/4 and must be included in the average.

Exercise 4.24.

Two signal energies are $1$ and $9$, with respective message probabilities $\tfrac34$ and $\tfrac14$. What is the average message energy?

(a) $3$
(b) $5$
(c) $\tfrac{15}{2}$

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

Weighting each energy by its probability gives $E=(3/4)1+(1/4)9=3$. The arithmetic mean 5 would assume equiprobable messages. The question asks average message energy, so no entropy or bit normalization is required.

Exercise 4.25.

Two equiprobable messages use the three-slot codewords $(-A,-A,-A)$ and $(A,A,A)$ on unit-energy orthogonal pulses. What positive $A$ gives $E_{\rm b}=6$?

(a) $\sqrt6$
(b) $2$
(c) $\sqrt2$

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

Each codeword carries one information bit and has energy $3A^2$. Thus $3A^2=6$ and $A=\sqrt2$. Repetition uses three coordinates but does not turn the binary message into three information bits.

Exercise 4.26.

Compare equiprobable pairs $\{(-A,-A),(A,A)\}$ and $\{(0,0),(B,B)\}$ on an orthonormal basis. What positive amplitudes give both pairs $E_{\rm b}=8$?

(a) $A=2,\ B=2\sqrt2$
(b) $A=2\sqrt2,\ B=2$
(c) $A=B=2$

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

The first pair has energy $2A^2$ for either message. The second has energies 0 and $2B^2$, whose mean is $B^2$. Each message carries one bit, so $2A^2=8$ and $B^2=8$. Equal average bit energy does not require equal individual message energies.

Exercise 4.27.

Four equiprobable messages each have energy $12$ and are represented with three orthonormal coordinates. Writing $E_{\rm s}$ for the average energy per coordinate and $E_{\rm b}$ for the energy per information bit, what are their values?

(a) $E_{\rm s}=4,\ E_{\rm b}=6$
(b) $E_{\rm s}=E_{\rm b}=12$
(c) $E_{\rm s}=6,\ E_{\rm b}=4$

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

There are three coordinates, so $E_{\rm s}=12/3=4$. There are four equiprobable messages, hence two bits per message, so $E_{\rm b}=12/2=6$. Coordinates and information bits count different quantities.

Exercise 4.28.

Two equiprobable codewords are $(0,0)$ and $(B,B)$, $B>0$, in an orthonormal basis. Subtract $(B/2,B/2)$ from both. What changes?

(a) The average energy and the distance both halve.
(b) The average energy halves and the distance stays the same.
(c) The average energy stays the same and the distance doubles.

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

The original average energy is $B^2$. The centered codewords are $(-B/2,-B/2)$ and $(B/2,B/2)$, each of energy $B^2/2$. Their difference remains $(B,B)$, so the distance is unchanged. A common offset costs energy without separating the messages.

Exercise 4.29.

White Gaussian noise has two-sided spectral density $N_0/2$ with $N_0=4$. What is the variance of its projection onto a unit-energy real pulse?

(a) $2$
(b) $4$
(c) $1$

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

Section 4.4 gives $\operatorname{Var}(\langle Z,\phi(t)\rangle)=(N_0/2)\|\phi(t)\|^2$. Unit energy and $N_0=4$ give variance 2. The spectral-density convention includes the factor 1/2 per real direction.

Exercise 4.30.

Let $u$ be a real pulse of energy $9$ and let white Gaussian noise have $N_0=2$. What is $\operatorname{Var}(\langle Z,u\rangle)$?

(a) $3$
(b) $9$
(c) $1$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

The projection variance is $(N_0/2)\|u\|^2=1\cdot9=9$. A non-unit test waveform scales the observed noise. Using variance $N_0/2$ alone would wrongly assume unit energy.

Exercise 4.31.

Let $u=1$ on $[0,1)$ and $v=1$ on $[\tfrac12,\tfrac32)$, zero elsewhere. White Gaussian noise has $N_0=2$. What is the covariance of its projections onto $u$ and $v$?

(a) $1$
(b) $\tfrac12$
(c) $0$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

The covariance is $(N_0/2)\langle u,v\rangle$. The product of the two pulses is 1 on the overlap of length 1/2, giving covariance 1/2. Both marginal variances are 1, but the overlapping directions are not orthogonal.

Exercise 4.32.

Two zero-mean projections of an arbitrary noise process have zero covariance. Without a joint Gaussian assumption, must they be independent?

(a) No, zero covariance alone does not establish independence.
(b) Yes, every pair of uncorrelated random variables is independent.
(c) Yes, provided both variances are positive.

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

The implication used in Section 4.4 relies on joint Gaussianity. For example, a variable $U$ uniform on $[-1,1]$ and $U^2-\mathrm E(U^2)$ are uncorrelated but dependent. Symmetry gives $\mathrm E(U)=\mathrm E(U^3)=0$, so the covariance is zero, but the second variable is a nonconstant function of the first. Orthogonal test directions therefore do not guarantee independent observations for every noise model.

Exercise 4.33.

White Gaussian noise with $N_0=4$ is projected onto an orthonormal pair, giving independent observations $Z_1,Z_2$. What is the variance of $Z_1+Z_2$?

(a) $8$
(b) $4$
(c) $2$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

Each projection has variance $N_0/2=2$. Independence gives zero covariance, so the sum variance is $2+2=4$. The standard deviations do not add when the variables are independent.

Exercise 4.34.

Let $Z_1,Z_2$ be independent Gaussian projections of variance $N_0/2$. What is the variance of $(Z_1-Z_2)/\sqrt2$?

(a) $\tfrac14N_0$
(b) $N_0$
(c) $N_0/2$

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

The unnormalized difference has variance $N_0$. Division by $\sqrt2$ divides the variance by 2. An orthonormal rotation preserves the white-noise variance per direction.

Exercise 4.35.

A transmitter sends $s(t)=\pm\sqrt E\,\phi(t)$, where $\phi(t)=1/\sqrt T$ on $[0,T)$. The receiver projects onto $u(t)=\sqrt{2/T}$ on $[0,T/2)$, zero elsewhere. In white Gaussian noise, what are the conditional mean and variance of $\langle Y(t),u(t)\rangle$?

(a) Mean $\pm\sqrt{E/2}$ and variance $N_0/4$.
(b) Mean $\pm\sqrt E$ and variance $N_0/2$.
(c) Mean $\pm\sqrt{E/2}$ and variance $N_0/2$.

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

The pulse $u(t)$ has unit energy, but $\langle\phi(t),u(t)\rangle=1/\sqrt2$. Thus the projected mean is $\pm\sqrt{E/2}$ while its noise variance remains $N_0/2$. The shorter correlator misses part of the signal without reducing normalized noise variance.

Exercise 4.36.

A transmitter sends $s(t)=3\phi(t)$, where $\phi(t)$ has unit energy. The receiver projects the received waveform onto $\phi(t)$. With white Gaussian noise and $N_0=4$, what are the mean and variance of this observation?

(a) Mean $3$ and variance $4$.
(b) Mean $3$ and variance $2$.
(c) Mean $9$ and variance $2$.

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

The signal projection is $3\langle\phi(t),\phi(t)\rangle=3$ because the pulse has unit energy. The noise projection has mean zero and variance $N_0/2=2$, by Section 4.4. The received observation therefore has mean $3$ and variance $2$.

Exercise 4.37.

Two equiprobable signals are $\pm\sqrt E\,\phi(t)$, with unit-energy $\phi(t)$. A receiver observes only the projection onto a unit-energy $u(t)$ orthogonal to $\phi(t)$. What is its minimum average error in white Gaussian noise?

(a) $\tfrac12$
(b) $\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{E/N_0})$
(c) $0$

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

The signal contributes zero in the observed direction under both messages. The two observation laws are identical pure-noise Gaussians, so observing them cannot distinguish equiprobable messages. Any decision rule achieves minimum average error 1/2.

Exercise 4.38.

White Gaussian noise with $N_0=6$ is projected onto two orthonormal pulses $\phi_1(t)$ and $\phi_2(t)$, giving independent observations $Z_1,Z_2$. What is the variance of $Z_1-Z_2$?

(a) $3$
(b) $0$
(c) $6$

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

Each noise projection has variance $N_0/2=3$. Independence makes the covariance zero, so $\operatorname{Var}(Z_1-Z_2)=3+3=6$. Subtracting independent noise observations adds their variances rather than canceling them.

Exercise 4.39.

A real two-coordinate Gaussian channel has independent noise projections with $N_0=2$. What is the conditional density at $\boldsymbol y$ given $\boldsymbol x$?

(a) $\tfrac1{2\pi}\exp(-\|\boldsymbol y-\boldsymbol x\|^2/4)$
(b) $\tfrac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp(-\|\boldsymbol y-\boldsymbol x\|^2/2)$
(c) $\tfrac1{2\pi}\exp(-\|\boldsymbol y-\boldsymbol x\|^2/2)$

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

Each real coordinate has variance $N_0/2=1$. Multiplying two unit-variance Gaussian densities gives the factor $1/(2\pi)$ and exponent $-\|\boldsymbol y-\boldsymbol x\|^2/2$. The square-root prefactor would normalize only one coordinate.

Exercise 4.40.

Two equiprobable codewords are $(0,0)$ and $(2,2)$ in an orthonormal basis. With white Gaussian noise, which codeword does ML choose at observation $(1,2)$?

(a) $(2,2)$
(b) $(0,0)$
(c) Both codewords are at the same distance.

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

Section 4.6 gives ML as the nearest-codeword rule. The squared distance to $(0,0)$ is $1^2+2^2=5$, whereas the squared distance to $(2,2)$ is $(1-2)^2+(2-2)^2=1$. The receiver therefore chooses $(2,2)$.

Exercise 4.41.

Two equiprobable signals have scalar coordinates $0$ and $2$ on a unit-energy basis, with additive zero-mean Gaussian noise. What is the ML threshold?

(a) $0$
(b) $1$
(c) $2$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

Comparing $y^2$ with $(y-2)^2$ gives the perpendicular bisector $y=1$. The larger coordinate is chosen above 1. The origin is not the boundary for an unequal-energy pair.

Exercise 4.42.

Two equiprobable codewords are $(1,1)$ and $(1,-1)$ in an orthonormal basis. With white Gaussian noise, which is preferred at observation $(-4,\tfrac15)$?

(a) $(1,1)$
(b) Both are equally likely.
(c) $(1,-1)$

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

The first-coordinate squared-distance term is 25 for both candidates and cancels. The second-coordinate contributions are $(1/5-1)^2=16/25$ and $(1/5+1)^2=36/25$, so (1,1) is nearer. A negative first observation does not change this comparison.

Exercise 4.43.

Scalar signals $0$ and $2$ have probabilities $\tfrac34$ and $\tfrac14$. Noise is zero-mean Gaussian with variance $1$. Above which threshold does MAP choose $2$?

(a) $1+\tfrac12\ln3$
(b) $1$
(c) $1-\tfrac12\ln3$

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

The likelihood ratio of 2 to 0 is $\exp(2y-2)$. MAP chooses 2 if this exceeds the prior ratio 3, giving $y>1+(\ln3)/2$. The more likely zero signal gets a larger decision region than under ML.

Exercise 4.44.

For equal-energy signals in white Gaussian noise, which rule is equivalent to ML?

(a) Maximize the absolute value of the correlation with the candidate signal.
(b) Minimize the correlation with the candidate signal.
(c) Maximize the correlation with the candidate signal.

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

Expanding squared distance leaves the score $\langle Y,s_m\rangle-E_m/2$. When all energies are equal, that energy term is common, so the correlation alone determines ML. Taking the absolute value loses the correlation sign and can confuse antipodal candidates. Equality of priors is needed only to identify this rule with MAP.

Exercise 4.45.

Two equiprobable scalar signals have coordinates $1$ and $3$ in zero-mean Gaussian noise. At $y=\tfrac32$, which comparison implements ML correctly?

(a) Use $yx$ alone and choose $x=3$.
(b) Use $yx-x^2/2$ and choose $x=1$.
(c) The scores $yx$ and $yx-\tfrac12x^2$ give the same decision.

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

The energy-corrected scores are $\tfrac32-\tfrac12=1$ for $x=1$ and $\tfrac92-\tfrac92=0$ for $x=3$. Hence ML chooses $x=1$, as the distances $\tfrac12$ and $\tfrac32$ confirm. Raw correlation is insufficient when signal energies differ.

Exercise 4.46.

Two equiprobable signals are $\pm2\phi(t)$ with unit-energy $\phi(t)$ and white Gaussian noise with $N_0=2$. What is the minimum average error?

(a) $\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt2)$
(b) $\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt2)$
(c) $\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}(1)$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

The signal distance is $d=4$. Section 4.6 gives $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}(d/(2\sqrt{N_0}))$, hence the argument is $4/(2\sqrt2)=\sqrt2$. Each message has energy 4, providing the same check through the antipodal formula.

Exercise 4.47.

Two equiprobable orthogonal signals each have energy $4$, and $N_0=2$. What is their ML error probability?

(a) $\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt2)$
(b) $\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}(1)$
(c) $\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}(2)$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

Orthogonality gives squared distance $4+4=8$. Therefore $d/(2\sqrt{N_0})=\sqrt8/(2\sqrt2)=1$. At the same bit energy the antipodal distance is larger, so its error is smaller.

Exercise 4.48.

Two equiprobable signals are $0$ and $3\phi(t)$, with unit-energy $\phi(t)$ and $N_0=1$. What is the ML error probability?

(a) $\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}(\tfrac32)$
(b) $\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}(\tfrac3{\sqrt2})$
(c) $\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}(3)$

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

The distance is 3 and the scalar threshold is 3/2, so the binary distance formula gives $\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}(3/2)$. The mean energy is 9/2, but inserting it into an antipodal formula would be wrong for this uncentered pair.

Exercise 4.49.

Four equiprobable signals have coordinates $(\pm1,\pm1)$, all sign combinations, in an orthonormal basis. With $N_0=2$, put $p=\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}(1/\sqrt2)$. What is the ML message-error probability?

(a) $p$
(b) $2p$
(c) $1-(1-p)^2$

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

The ML receiver decides each sign independently. Each coordinate has variance 1 and error p. A message is correct only when both signs are correct, with probability $(1-p)^2$. The expression 2p double-counts simultaneous errors and is only an upper bound.

Exercise 4.50.

In an orthonormal plane, an equiprobable pair $(2,0),(0,2)$ is replaced by $(3,1),(1,3)$. In the same white Gaussian noise, how does the optimal error change?

(a) It increases because neither signal lies on an axis.
(b) It decreases because both signals now have more energy.
(c) It stays unchanged although average energy increases.

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

The new pair is the old one translated by (1,1). Both difference vectors have squared norm 8, so binary ML error is unchanged. Average message energy increases from 4 to 10. Energy in a common offset need not improve separation or reliability.
