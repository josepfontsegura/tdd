Test 4. Continuous time

Download PDF

The ten questions of the test, each with two options and one correct answer, in the order of the published file; the printed copies had the questions and the options in a different order. Work out your answer before opening a solution.

Question 1. Two signals x and y are orthogonal

(a) when \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(t)\,y(t)\drm t=0, whether or not they overlap in time.
(b) only when they do not overlap in time.

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

Definition 4.2. The two pulses of Example 4.3 overlap on the whole interval and are orthogonal.

Question 2. A set of two signals \{\phi_1,\phi_2\} is orthonormal when the two signals are orthogonal and

(a) have the same energy, whatever its value.
(b) each has unit energy.

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

Definition 4.2: unit energy for every signal and a zero inner product for every pair.

Question 3. Let x(t)=x_1\phi_1(t)+x_2\phi_2(t), where \{\phi_1,\phi_2\} is orthonormal. The coordinate x_1 is obtained from the signal as

(a) \langle x,\phi_1\rangle/\|x\|.
(b) \langle x,\phi_1\rangle.

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

Proposition 4.4(a): x_i=\langle x,\phi_i\rangle, with no normalization, because the basis signals have unit energy.

Question 4. Let x(t)=x_1\phi_1(t)+x_2\phi_2(t), where \{\phi_1,\phi_2\} is orthonormal. The energy of x is

(a) x_1^2+x_2^2.
(b) (x_1+x_2)^2.

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

Proposition 4.4(c): the cross term 2x_1x_2\langle\phi_1,\phi_2\rangle vanishes, as in the energy check of lecture 7.

Question 5. Let \bar x be the projection of a signal x onto a signal space that does not contain x, and let e=x-\bar x be the error. Then

(a) e is orthogonal to every signal of the space.
(b) e is itself a signal of the space.

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

Proposition 4.6: the error is orthogonal to the space, and therefore \|x\|^2=\|\bar x\|^2+\|e\|^2.

Question 6. A transmitter sends one of M waveforms, which span a signal space of dimension n. One channel use of the Gaussian channel of the first topics corresponds to

(a) one of the M waveforms.
(b) one of the n orthonormal directions of the signal space.

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

By (4.11) and Table 4.1, the Gaussian channel is used n times, once per dimension.

Question 7. Let \{\phi_1,\phi_2\} be orthonormal. The noise projections Z_1=\langle Z,\phi_1\rangle and Z_2=\langle Z,\phi_2\rangle are independent Gaussian random variables of zero mean, each with variance

(a) \No/2.
(b) \No.

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

Proposition 4.10; \No is the variance of the sum Z_1+Z_2.

Question 8. Two signals of the space spanned by an orthonormal basis have coordinate vectors \x and \y. The distance between the two signals

(a) depends also on the shape of the basis signals.
(b) equals the distance \|\x-\y\| between the coordinate vectors.

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

Proposition 4.4(d): signals and coordinate vectors share energies and distances.

Question 9. The receiver that chooses the message whose coordinate vector is nearest to (Y_1,\ldots,Y_n) is guaranteed to have the smallest error probability when

(a) the waveforms have the same energy.
(b) the messages are equiprobable.

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

Section 4.6: for equiprobable messages MAP is ML, the nearest point, also at unequal energies.

Question 10. As in Seminar 6, two equiprobable messages have the coordinate vectors (c,c) and (c,-c), with c>0, and the receiver computes (Y_1,Y_2). The optimal receiver decides the first message when

(a) Y_2>0.
(b) Y_1+Y_2>0.

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

The two points differ only in the second coordinate, so the nearest one is given by the sign of Y_2.
