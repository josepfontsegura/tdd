Exercises: 1. Detection

Work out your answer before opening a solution. Each exercise has three options and exactly one correct answer; the solution explains the choice.

For additive channels, noise is independent of the input unless stated otherwise. We use ${\rm Re}(X)$ and ${\rm Im}(X)$ for real and imaginary parts, $\ln$ for the natural logarithm, and $\operatorname{erfc}(u)=\frac2{\sqrt\pi}\int_u^\infty e^{-t^2}\,dt$ for the complementary error function. Answers may be left in terms of $\operatorname{erfc}$. Assign probability-zero decision boundaries arbitrarily; for ties with positive probability, follow the rule in the exercise. Symbols in Gaussian and uniform-noise examples represent amplitudes, with $E_{\rm s}=\mathrm E|X|^2$ and SNR equal to energy divided by total noise variance. Exponential-channel inputs represent energies, with the mean-energy convention stated in each exercise.

Exercise 1.1.

A bit-flipping channel (BSC) has crossover probability $p=\tfrac1{10}$, and the input bit is $0$ with probability $\Pr(X=0)=\tfrac45$. What does the MAP detector decide when $y=1$ is received, and what is the posterior probability $\Pr(X=1\mid Y=1)$?

(a) It decides $\hat x=0$, and $\Pr(X=1\mid Y=1)=\tfrac{1}{5}$.
(b) It decides $\hat x=1$, and $\Pr(X=1\mid Y=1)=\tfrac{9}{10}$.
(c) It decides $\hat x=1$, and $\Pr(X=1\mid Y=1)=\tfrac{9}{13}$.

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

The MAP detector compares the joint scores $\Pr(x)W(y|x)$. At $y=1$ they are $\tfrac45\cdot\tfrac1{10}=\tfrac{2}{25}$ for $x=0$ and $\tfrac15\cdot\tfrac9{10}=\tfrac{9}{50}$ for $x=1$, so MAP decides $\hat x=1$ despite the unequal priors. At $y=0$ the scores are $\tfrac{18}{25}$ and $\tfrac1{50}$, so MAP decides $0$. Hence MAP and ML coincide, $g(y)=y$, with $P_{\rm e}(0)=P_{\rm e}(1)=P_{\rm e}=\tfrac1{10}$.

By Bayes' rule, $\Pr(X=1\mid Y=1)$ is the score of $x=1$ divided by $\Pr(Y=1)=\tfrac2{25}+\tfrac9{50}=\tfrac{13}{50}$, which gives $\tfrac{9/50}{13/50}=\tfrac9{13}$. Option (a) uses the prior alone, and option (b) confuses the likelihood $W(1|1)=\tfrac9{10}$ with the posterior: an output does not identify its input with certainty even when MAP and ML agree.

Exercise 1.2.

A bit-flipping channel (BSC) has crossover probability $p=\tfrac1{10}$, and the input bit is $0$ with probability $\Pr(X=0)=\tfrac{19}{20}$. What are the average error probability $P_{\rm e}$ of the MAP detector and its conditional error probability $P_{\rm e}(1)$ when $X=1$ is sent?

(a) $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac1{20}$ and $P_{\rm e}(1)=\tfrac1{10}$
(b) $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac1{20}$ and $P_{\rm e}(1)=1$
(c) $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac1{10}$ and $P_{\rm e}(1)=\tfrac1{10}$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

At $y=0$ the joint scores $\Pr(x)W(y|x)$ for $x=0,1$ are $\tfrac{171}{200}$ and $\tfrac{1}{200}$; at $y=1$ they are $\tfrac{19}{200}$ and $\tfrac{9}{200}$. In both cases $x=0$ wins, so the MAP detector always decides $0$: $\mathcal D_0=\{0,1\}$ and $\mathcal D_1=\varnothing$. It is never wrong when $0$ is sent and always wrong when $1$ is sent, $P_{\rm e}(0)=0$ and $P_{\rm e}(1)=1$, so $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac{19}{20}\cdot 0+\tfrac1{20}\cdot 1=\tfrac1{20}$.

The ML detector $g(y)=y$ has $P_{\rm e}(0)=P_{\rm e}(1)=P_{\rm e}=\tfrac1{10}$, option (c). MAP halves the average error, but it makes every occurrence of the rare input an error. Minimizing the average error does not minimize each conditional error separately; option (a) mixes the two detectors.

Exercise 1.3.

A bit-flipping channel (BSC) has crossover probability $p=\tfrac45$ and equiprobable inputs. Which is the ML detector, and what is its average error probability $P_{\rm e}$?

(a) $g(y)=1-y$, with $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac15$
(b) $g(y)=0$ for both outputs, with $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac12$
(c) $g(y)=y$, with $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac45$

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

Since $p=\tfrac45>\tfrac12$, the likelihood $W(y|x)$ is larger for $x\neq y$: the channel flips more often than it preserves, and the ML detector flips the received bit, $g(y)=1-y$, with regions $\mathcal D_0=\{1\}$ and $\mathcal D_1=\{0\}$. It errs when the channel does not flip, so $P_{\rm e}(0)=P_{\rm e}(1)=\tfrac15$ and $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac15$. With equiprobable inputs this is also the MAP detector, hence no detector does better.

The detector $g(y)=y$ has conditional errors $(\tfrac45,\tfrac45)$ and average $\tfrac45$, option (c). The constant detector of option (b) has $\mathcal D_0=\{0,1\}$ and $\mathcal D_1=\varnothing$, conditional errors $(0,1)$ and average $\tfrac12$. It is legitimate for a detector to assign an empty region to a symbol, but here it is far from optimal.

Exercise 1.4.

A binary asymmetric channel has $W(1\mid0)=\tfrac15$ and $W(0\mid1)=\tfrac25$, and the input bit is $0$ with probability $\Pr(X=0)=\tfrac45$. What is the average error probability $P_{\rm e}$ of the MAP detector?

(a) $\tfrac{6}{25}$
(b) $\tfrac15$
(c) $\tfrac12$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

The missing transition probabilities are $W(0\mid0)=\tfrac45$ and $W(1\mid1)=\tfrac35$. The joint scores $\Pr(x)W(y|x)$ for $x=0,1$ are $\tfrac{16}{25}$ and $\tfrac{2}{25}$ at $y=0$, and $\tfrac{4}{25}$ and $\tfrac{3}{25}$ at $y=1$. The MAP detector decides $0$ for both outputs, so $P_{\rm e}(0)=0$, $P_{\rm e}(1)=1$ and $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac45\cdot0+\tfrac15\cdot1=\tfrac15$. Summing the scores column by column gives the output distribution, $\Pr(Y=0)=\tfrac{18}{25}$ and $\Pr(Y=1)=\tfrac{7}{25}$.

The ML detector decides $g(y)=y$, since $\tfrac45>\tfrac25$ and $\tfrac35>\tfrac15$, with $P_{\rm e}(0)=\tfrac15$, $P_{\rm e}(1)=\tfrac25$ and $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac45\cdot\tfrac15+\tfrac15\cdot\tfrac25=\tfrac{6}{25}$, option (a), which is larger as it must be. Option (c) averages the two conditional errors of MAP with equal weights instead of the priors.

Exercise 1.5.

A Z-channel has $W(0\mid0)=1$, $W(0\mid1)=\tfrac25$ and $W(1\mid1)=\tfrac35$, and the input bit is $0$ with probability $\Pr(X=0)=\tfrac14$. What is the posterior probability $\Pr(X=0\mid Y=0)$?

(a) $\tfrac{5}{11}$
(b) $\tfrac{5}{7}$
(c) $1$

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

At $y=0$ the joint scores $\Pr(x)W(0|x)$ are $\tfrac14\cdot1=\tfrac14$ for $x=0$ and $\tfrac34\cdot\tfrac25=\tfrac{3}{10}$ for $x=1$. Their sum is $\Pr(Y=0)=\tfrac{11}{20}$, so by Bayes' rule $\Pr(X=0\mid Y=0)=\tfrac{1/4}{11/20}=\tfrac5{11}$ and $\Pr(X=1\mid Y=0)=\tfrac6{11}$. An output $1$ can only come from input $1$, so $\Pr(X=1\mid Y=1)=1$, but an output $0$ is not an unambiguous input $0$: option (c) is wrong. Option (b) normalizes the likelihoods $1$ and $\tfrac25$ without the prior.

Since $\tfrac6{11}>\tfrac5{11}$, the MAP detector decides $1$ at both outputs, with conditional errors $(1,0)$ and $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac14$. The ML detector decides the received bit, because $1>\tfrac25$ at $y=0$, with conditional errors $(0,\tfrac25)$ and $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac34\cdot\tfrac25=\tfrac{3}{10}$.

Exercise 1.6.

A channel with binary input first erases the bit with probability $\tfrac15$, producing the output $?$. Conditional on no erasure, it flips the bit with probability $\tfrac1{10}$. These probabilities are the same for either input, and $\Pr(X=0)=\tfrac35$. The receiver reports each unerased bit as received and replaces every erasure by the MAP decision at $y=?$. What is the average bit error probability $P_{\rm e}$ of this receiver?

(a) $\tfrac{4}{25}$
(b) $\tfrac{7}{25}$
(c) $\tfrac{2}{25}$

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

In output order $0,1,?$, the transition rows are $(\tfrac{18}{25},\tfrac{2}{25},\tfrac15)$ for $x=0$ and $(\tfrac{2}{25},\tfrac{18}{25},\tfrac15)$ for $x=1$. At an erasure both likelihoods equal $\tfrac15$, so the MAP decision is the more probable bit, $0$. At $y=0,1$ the joint scores are $(\tfrac{54}{125},\tfrac{4}{125})$ and $(\tfrac{6}{125},\tfrac{36}{125})$, so reporting the received bit is the MAP decision there too. When $0$ is sent, an error requires a flip without erasure, $P_{\rm e}(0)=\tfrac{2}{25}$. When $1$ is sent, both a flip and an erasure lead to a wrong bit, $P_{\rm e}(1)=\tfrac{2}{25}+\tfrac15=\tfrac{7}{25}$. The average is $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac35\cdot\tfrac{2}{25}+\tfrac25\cdot\tfrac{7}{25}=\tfrac{4}{25}$.

Before the erasures are filled, the probability of an incorrect reported bit is $\tfrac45\cdot\tfrac1{10}=\tfrac{2}{25}$, option (c), and the erasure probability is $\tfrac15$; counting every erasure as a failure gives $\tfrac{7}{25}$, option (b). An erasure, an incorrect reported bit and an incorrect bit after filling are three different events.

Exercise 1.7.

An asymmetric erasure channel has $W(?\mid0)=\tfrac45$, $W(0\mid0)=\tfrac15$, $W(?\mid1)=\tfrac15$ and $W(1\mid1)=\tfrac45$; all other transition probabilities are zero. The input bit is $0$ with probability $\Pr(X=0)=\tfrac35$. What is the posterior probability $\Pr(X=0\mid Y=?)$ at an erasure?

(a) $\tfrac{3}{5}$
(b) $\tfrac{4}{5}$
(c) $\tfrac{6}{7}$

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

At $y=?$ the joint scores $\Pr(x)W(?|x)$ are $\tfrac35\cdot\tfrac45=\tfrac{12}{25}$ for $x=0$ and $\tfrac25\cdot\tfrac15=\tfrac{2}{25}$ for $x=1$. Their sum is $\Pr(Y=?)=\tfrac{14}{25}$, so $\Pr(X=0\mid Y=?)=\tfrac{12}{14}=\tfrac67$. This differs from the prior $\tfrac35$ of option (a): the erasure itself is informative, because input $0$ is erased more often. Option (b) is the likelihood $W(?\mid0)$, which ignores the prior.

The ordinary outputs $0$ and $1$ identify the input without error. At an erasure, ML decides $0$ because $\tfrac45>\tfrac15$, and MAP also decides $0$. Both detectors have conditional errors $(0,\tfrac15)$ and $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac25\cdot\tfrac15=\tfrac{2}{25}$, which is much smaller than the erasure probability $\tfrac{14}{25}$.

Exercise 1.8.

Inputs and outputs take values in $\{0,1,2\}$ and the channel matrix, with rows indexed by the input, is $$W=\begin{pmatrix}3/5&3/10&1/10\\1/5&1/2&3/10\\1/10&1/5&7/10\end{pmatrix}.$$ The input probabilities are $(\tfrac1{10},\tfrac7{10},\tfrac15)$. What is the average error probability $P_{\rm e}$ of the MAP detector?

(a) $\tfrac{3}{10}$
(b) $\tfrac{9}{20}$
(c) $\tfrac{2}{3}$

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

The joint scores $\Pr(x)W(y|x)$, with rows indexed by $x$ and columns by $y$, are $(0.06,0.03,0.01)$, $(0.14,0.35,0.21)$ and $(0.02,0.04,0.14)$. The middle row is the largest in every column, so the MAP detector decides $\hat x=1$ whatever is received. Its conditional errors are $(1,0,1)$ and $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac1{10}+\tfrac15=\tfrac{3}{10}$. At $y=2$, for instance, $\Pr(Y=2)=0.36$ and the largest posterior is $\Pr(X=1\mid Y=2)=\tfrac{0.21}{0.36}=\tfrac{7}{12}$.

Each column of $W$ has its largest entry on the diagonal, so the ML detector decides $g(y)=y$, with conditional errors $(\tfrac25,\tfrac12,\tfrac3{10})$ and $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac1{25}+\tfrac7{20}+\tfrac3{50}=\tfrac{9}{20}$, option (b). Option (c) averages the three conditional errors of MAP with equal weights instead of the input probabilities.

Exercise 1.9.

The inputs $0$ and $1$ are equiprobable and the outputs take values in $\{0,1,2\}$. The channel rows are $W(\cdot\mid0)=(\tfrac12,\tfrac15,\tfrac3{10})$ and $W(\cdot\mid1)=(\tfrac1{10},\tfrac35,\tfrac3{10})$. A colleague proposes the detector that decides $0$ only when $y=1$ and decides $1$ otherwise. What are its conditional error probabilities $(P_{\rm e}(0),P_{\rm e}(1))$ and its average error probability $P_{\rm e}$?

(a) $(\tfrac12,\tfrac1{10})$ and $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac{3}{10}$
(b) $(\tfrac45,\tfrac35)$ and $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac{7}{10}$
(c) $(\tfrac15,\tfrac25)$ and $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac{3}{10}$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

First identify the decision regions: $\mathcal D_0=\{1\}$ and $\mathcal D_1=\{0,2\}$. Then sum the channel law over the outputs outside the region of the transmitted symbol: $P_{\rm e}(0)=W(0\mid0)+W(2\mid0)=\tfrac12+\tfrac3{10}=\tfrac45$ and $P_{\rm e}(1)=W(1\mid1)=\tfrac35$. The average is $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac45+\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac35=\tfrac{7}{10}$, worse than deciding at random.

The ML detector decides $0$ at $y=0$, $1$ at $y=1$, and faces a tie at $y=2$. Breaking the tie toward $0$ gives conditional errors $(\tfrac15,\tfrac25)$ and $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac{3}{10}$, option (c). Nearest-point detection on the numerical alphabet, which assigns $y=2$ to $1$, gives $(\tfrac12,\tfrac1{10})$ and also $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac{3}{10}$, option (a); it is the other ML tie-breaking rule. Equal average errors need not mean equal conditional errors.

Exercise 1.10.

Equiprobable symbols $X\in\{-2,+2\}$ are sent over the real Gaussian channel $Y=X+Z$ with $Z\sim\mathcal N(0,2)$, that is, noise variance $\sigma^2=2$. The receiver uses the ML detector. What is the average error probability $P_{\rm e}$?

(a) $\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt2)$
(b) $\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}(1)$
(c) $\operatorname{erfc}(1)$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

With equiprobable symbols MAP and ML coincide, and in Gaussian noise ML is the nearest-point rule: the threshold is at $y=0$, with $\mathcal D_{-2}=(-\infty,0)$ and $\mathcal D_{+2}=(0,\infty)$; the value $y=0$ has probability zero and may be assigned to either symbol. When $x=-2$ is sent, an error occurs when $Z>2$; when $x=+2$ is sent, when $Z<-2$. For a real Gaussian of variance $\sigma^2$, $\Pr(Z>a)=\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(a/(\sqrt2\sigma)\bigr)$, and here $a=2$ and $\sqrt2\sigma=2$, so each conditional error is $\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}(1)\approx 0.079$. The average is the same, $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}(1)$.

The symbol energy is $E_{\rm s}=4$ and $\mathrm{SNR}=E_{\rm s}/\sigma^2=2$, consistent with $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\sqrt{\mathrm{SNR}/2}\bigr)$. Option (a) uses $a/\sigma$ instead of $a/(\sqrt2\sigma)$ in the argument, which is the complex-channel formula. Option (c) adds the two error regions, but each symbol has only one error region: the regions $Z>2$ and $Z<-2$ belong to different transmitted symbols and are not summed.

Exercise 1.11.

On-off signaling $X\in\{0,3\}$ with $\Pr(X=0)=\tfrac34$ and $\Pr(X=3)=\tfrac14$ is used over the real Gaussian channel $Y=X+Z$ with $Z\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$. The MAP detector decides $\hat x=3$ when $y>t$ and $\hat x=0$ otherwise. What is the threshold $t$?

(a) $t=\tfrac32+\tfrac13\ln 3$
(b) $t=\tfrac32-\tfrac13\ln 3$
(c) $t=\tfrac32+\tfrac16\ln 3$

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

MAP decides $3$ when $\tfrac14 e^{-(y-3)^2/2}>\tfrac34 e^{-y^2/2}$. Taking logarithms, the terms in $y^2$ cancel and the inequality becomes $3y-\tfrac92>\ln 3$, that is, $y>t=\tfrac32+\tfrac13\ln 3\approx 1.87$. The threshold lies above the ML midpoint $\tfrac32$: the region of the more probable symbol $0$ grows. The shift is $\sigma^2\ln 3$ divided by the distance $3$ between the symbols, not by twice the symbol value.

The error events are $Z>t$ for $x=0$ and $t-3>Z$ for $x=3$, so $P_{\rm e}(0)=\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac{t}{\sqrt2}\bigr)$, $P_{\rm e}(3)=\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac{3-t}{\sqrt2}\bigr)$ and $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac38\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac{t}{\sqrt2}\bigr)+\tfrac18\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac{3-t}{\sqrt2}\bigr)\approx 0.055$, below the ML value $\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac{3}{2\sqrt2}\bigr)\approx 0.067$. The average energy is $E_{\rm s}=\tfrac14\cdot 9=\tfrac94$, so $\mathrm{SNR}=\tfrac94$.

Exercise 1.12.

The symbols $X\in\{-2,0,2\}$ with probabilities $\bigl(\tfrac14,\tfrac12,\tfrac14\bigr)$ are sent through the real Gaussian channel $Y=X+Z$ with noise variance $\sigma^2=2$. The receiver uses the nearest-point detector. What is the average error probability $P_{\rm e}$?

(a) $\tfrac23\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac12\bigr)$
(b) $\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac12\bigr)$
(c) $\tfrac34\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac12\bigr)$

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

The thresholds are the midpoints $-1$ and $1$, at distance $1$ from every symbol. Each tail has probability $\Pr(Z>1)=\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2\sigma}\bigr)=\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac12\bigr)$. The outer symbols have one error tail each, so $P_{\rm e}(\pm2)=\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac12\bigr)$. For $x=0$ the error set in noise coordinates is $(-\infty,-1)\cup(1,\infty)$, two disjoint tails, so $P_{\rm e}(0)=\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac12\bigr)$, even though this symbol carries no energy.

Averaging with the priors, $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac14\cdot\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac12\bigr)+\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac12\bigr)+\tfrac14\cdot\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac12\bigr)=\tfrac34\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac12\bigr)\approx 0.36$. Counting one tail for the middle symbol gives $\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac12\bigr)$; averaging with equal weights gives $\tfrac23\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac12\bigr)$. The energy is $E_{\rm s}=\tfrac14\cdot4+\tfrac14\cdot4=2$, so $\mathrm{SNR}=1$.

Exercise 1.13.

The symbols $X\in\{-2,0,2\}$ with probabilities $\bigl(\tfrac14,\tfrac12,\tfrac14\bigr)$ are sent through $Y=X+Z$ with $Z\sim\mathcal N(0,2)$. The receiver uses the MAP detector. What is the decision region $\mathcal D_0$ of the symbol $0$?

(a) $\mathcal D_0=(-1,1)$
(b) $\mathcal D_0=(-1-\ln 2,\,1+\ln 2)$
(c) $\mathcal D_0=(-1+\ln 2,\,1-\ln 2)$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

For $y>0$ the symbol $-2$ cannot beat $2$, so it suffices to compare $0$ and $2$: MAP decides $2$ when $\tfrac14 e^{-(y-2)^2/4}>\tfrac12 e^{-y^2/4}$. Taking logarithms, $y-1>\ln 2$, so the boundary is $b=1+\ln 2\approx 1.69$, and by symmetry the other boundary is $-b$. Hence $\mathcal D_0=(-b,b)$, wider than the nearest-point region $(-1,1)$: the prior favors the middle symbol, and the shift is $\sigma^2\ln 2$ divided by the distance $2$. Shrinking the region, as in $(-1+\ln 2,1-\ln 2)$, moves the boundaries in the wrong direction.

With noise variance $2$, each tail at distance $a$ has probability $\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac a2\bigr)$. Therefore $P_{\rm e}(0)=\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac b2\bigr)$, $P_{\rm e}(\pm2)=\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac{2-b}{2}\bigr)$ and $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac b2\bigr)+\tfrac14\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac{2-b}{2}\bigr)\approx 0.32$, smaller than the nearest-point value $\tfrac34\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac12\bigr)\approx 0.36$. The middle error decreases, the outer errors increase, and the weighted average cannot exceed the nearest-point value because MAP minimizes it.

Exercise 1.14.

Equiprobable symbols $X\in\{-3,0,1\}$ are sent through the real Gaussian channel $Y=X+Z$ with $Z\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$, and the receiver uses the nearest-point detector. What is the average error probability $P_{\rm e}$?

(a) $\tfrac13\Bigl[\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac{3}{2\sqrt2}\bigr)+\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2\sqrt2}\bigr)\Bigr]$
(b) $\tfrac23\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2\sqrt2}\bigr)$
(c) $\tfrac13\Bigl[\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac{3}{2\sqrt2}\bigr)+\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2\sqrt2}\bigr)\Bigr]$

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

The thresholds are the midpoints $-\tfrac32$ and $\tfrac12$, so the regions are $(-\infty,-\tfrac32)$, $(-\tfrac32,\tfrac12)$ and $(\tfrac12,\infty)$. The outer symbols have one tail each: $P_{\rm e}(-3)=\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac{3}{2\sqrt2}\bigr)$ and $P_{\rm e}(1)=\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2\sqrt2}\bigr)$. The middle symbol has two tails at different distances, $\tfrac32$ on the left and $\tfrac12$ on the right, so $P_{\rm e}(0)=\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac{3}{2\sqrt2}\bigr)+\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2\sqrt2}\bigr)$.

Averaging the three conditional errors gives $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac13\bigl[\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac{3}{2\sqrt2}\bigr)+\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2\sqrt2}\bigr)\bigr]\approx 0.25$. Counting only the nearer tail of the middle symbol loses $\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac{3}{2\sqrt2}\bigr)$; using the smallest spacing for every tail gives the uniform 3-PAM formula $\tfrac23\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2\sqrt2}\bigr)$, which is far too large. The energy is $E_{\rm s}=\tfrac13(9+0+1)=\tfrac{10}{3}$, so $\mathrm{SNR}=\tfrac{10}{3}$.

Exercise 1.15.

Equiprobable symbols $X\in\{-1,1\}$ are sent through the real Gaussian channel $Y=X+Z$ with $Z\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$. The receiver decides $\hat x=1$ if $y>1$ and $\hat x=-1$ otherwise. What is its average error probability $P_{\rm e}$?

(a) $\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\sqrt2\bigr)$
(b) $\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\bigr)$
(c) $\tfrac14+\tfrac14\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\sqrt2\bigr)$

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

The threshold sits on the symbol $1$. When $x=-1$ is sent, an error requires $Z>2$, so $P_{\rm e}(-1)=\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac{2}{\sqrt2}\bigr)=\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\sqrt2\bigr)$. When $x=1$ is sent, an error requires $Z\leq 0$, which has probability $\tfrac12$. Hence $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\sqrt2\bigr)+\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac12=\tfrac14+\tfrac14\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\sqrt2\bigr)\approx 0.26$. The small tail of one symbol is paid with a coin flip on the other.

For a general threshold $t$, $P_{\rm e}(t)=\tfrac14\bigl[\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac{1+t}{\sqrt2}\bigr)+\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac{1-t}{\sqrt2}\bigr)\bigr]$, minimized at $t=0$, the ML threshold, where it equals $\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\bigr)\approx 0.16$. The value $\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\sqrt2\bigr)$ would require both symbols to be at distance $2$ from the threshold.

Exercise 1.16.

Equiprobable symbols $X\in\{-1,1\}$ are sent through $Y=X+Z$ with $Z\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$. A window detector decides $\hat x=1$ when $|y|<1$ and $\hat x=-1$ otherwise, so that $\mathcal D_1=(-1,1)$ and $\mathcal D_{-1}=(-\infty,-1]\cup[1,\infty)$. What is its average error probability $P_{\rm e}$?

(a) $\tfrac12+\tfrac14\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\sqrt2\bigr)$
(b) $\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\bigr)$
(c) $\tfrac12$

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

When $x=1$ is sent, $Y=1+Z$ leaves the window $(-1,1)$ when $Z\leq-2$ or $Z\geq0$, two disjoint events, so $P_{\rm e}(1)=\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\sqrt2\bigr)+\tfrac12$. When $x=-1$ is sent, an error means entering the window, $2>Z>0$, so $P_{\rm e}(-1)=\tfrac12-\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\sqrt2\bigr)$. Forgetting the upper limit $Z<2$ gives $P_{\rm e}(-1)=\tfrac12$ and the wrong average $\tfrac12+\tfrac14\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\sqrt2\bigr)$.

The two $\operatorname{erfc}$ terms cancel in the average: $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac12\bigl[P_{\rm e}(1)+P_{\rm e}(-1)\bigr]=\tfrac12$ exactly. The detector reacts to the observation, but with equiprobable inputs it performs no better than always answering the same symbol, and far worse than the ML threshold at $0$, whose error is $\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\bigr)\approx0.16$.

Exercise 1.17.

Exercise 1.18.

Equiprobable symbols $X\in\{\pm2\pm j\}$ are sent over the complex Gaussian channel with circular noise of total variance $\sigma^2=2$, and the receiver uses the nearest-point detector. What is the symbol error probability $P_{\rm e}$?

(a) $1-\bigl[1-\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\sqrt2\bigr)\bigr]\bigl[1-\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\bigr)\bigr]$
(b) $1-\bigl[1-\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}(1)\bigr]\bigl[1-\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac12\bigr)\bigr]$
(c) $\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\sqrt2\bigr)+\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\bigr)$

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

The perpendicular bisectors are the two axes, so each symbol owns a quadrant. The real and imaginary parts of the noise are independent Gaussians of variance $\sigma^2/2=1$ each. The real component crosses its boundary at distance $2$ with probability $p_R=\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac{2}{\sqrt2}\bigr)=\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\sqrt2\bigr)$, and the imaginary component at distance $1$ with probability $p_I=\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\bigr)$. Using the total variance $2$ for each component would give the arguments $1$ and $\tfrac12$ instead.

The plain sum $p_R+p_I$ counts twice the outputs that cross both boundaries. The two component errors differ because the rectangle has unequal sides. Each symbol has energy $4+1=5$, so $E_{\rm s}=5$ and $\mathrm{SNR}=\tfrac52$.

At an erasure both likelihoods equal $\varepsilon$: any choice is ML, and the MAP detector compares $q\varepsilon$ with $(1-q)\varepsilon$ and decides the more probable bit. It errs only when the other bit was sent and erased, so $P_{\rm e}=\varepsilon\min(q,1-q)$.

Breaking the ML tie toward $0$ gives conditional errors $(0,\varepsilon)$ and average $(1-q)\varepsilon$; toward $1$ it gives $(\varepsilon,0)$ and $q\varepsilon$. The MAP detector picks the smaller of the two, and $\tfrac12\varepsilon$ is the value for every tie-breaking rule only when $q=\tfrac12$. Choosing the less probable bit at the erasure gives the worst value, $\varepsilon\max(q,1-q)$.

For input $1$, a wrong bit requires $Z<-\tfrac32$, with probability $w=\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\!\bigl(\tfrac{3}{2\sqrt2}\bigr)\approx0.067$; an erasure requires $-\tfrac32\le Z\le-\tfrac12$, with probability $\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\!\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2\sqrt2}\bigr)-w\approx0.242$; a correct bit requires $Z>-\tfrac12$, with probability $c=1-\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\!\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2\sqrt2}\bigr)\approx0.691$. By symmetry the same three numbers hold for input $-1$, so they are the unconditional probabilities and they sum to one. Conditioning on a bit being reported, $\Pr(\text{wrong}\mid\text{bit reported})=\tfrac{w}{w+c}\approx0.088$.

Option (c) is the unconditional probability $w$ of a wrong bit, without the conditioning. Option (a) counts every erasure as a failure, $w+\text{erasure}=\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\!\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2\sqrt2}\bigr)\approx0.309$, which is larger than the error $\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\!\bigl(\tfrac1{\sqrt2}\bigr)\approx0.159$ of ordinary nearest-point detection. Abstaining makes the retained decisions more reliable at the cost of discarding about $24\%$ of the observations.

Exercise 1.49.

Equiprobable symbols $X\in\{-a,a\}$, with $a>0$ a design parameter, are sent with additive noise uniform on $[-b,b]$, $b>0$ fixed, and nearest-point detection. The noise variance is $b^2/3$. What is the smallest SNR $=E_{\rm s}/\operatorname{Var}(Z)$ that guarantees $P_{\rm e}\le\tfrac1{20}$?

(a) $\mathrm{SNR}=\tfrac{27}{10}$
(b) $\mathrm{SNR}=\tfrac{243}{100}$
(c) $\mathrm{SNR}=\tfrac{81}{100}$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

The boundary is $0$. For input $a$ the error requires $Z<-a$, a tail of length $b-a$ out of $2b$ when $a < b$, and an event of probability zero when $a\ge b$. By symmetry the same holds for $-a$, so $P_{\rm e}=\max\{0,\tfrac{b-a}{2b}\}$. The target $\tfrac{b-a}{2b}\le\tfrac1{20}$ requires $a\ge\tfrac9{10}b$, hence the smallest symbol energy is $E_{\rm s}=a^2=\tfrac{81}{100}b^2$ and the smallest SNR is $\tfrac{81b^2/100}{b^2/3}=\tfrac{243}{100}=2.43$.

Option (c) is the ratio $a^2/b^2$, which forgets that the variance of the uniform noise is $b^2/3$ and not $b^2$; option (a) multiplies by $3$ the ratio $a/b$ instead of its square. For comparison, zero error first occurs at $a=b$, that is, at $\mathrm{SNR}=3$: the shifted supports then touch only at a point of probability zero, so the tie assignment does not matter.

Exercise 1.50.

A binary asymmetric channel has $W(1|0)=\tfrac1{10}$ and $W(0|1)=\tfrac3{10}$, and the prior $\Pr(X=0)=q$ varies over $0\le q\le1$. For each $q$ the MAP detector is used. Which prior makes the MAP error probability largest, and what is that error?

(a) $q=\tfrac78$, with $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac18$
(b) $q=\tfrac12$, with $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac15$
(c) $q=\tfrac14$, with $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac14$

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

At output $0$, MAP compares $\tfrac9{10}q$ with $\tfrac3{10}(1-q)$ and chooses $0$ when $q>\tfrac14$. At output $1$, it compares $\tfrac1{10}q$ with $\tfrac7{10}(1-q)$ and chooses $0$ when $q>\tfrac78$. Hence MAP always decides $1$ for $q<\tfrac14$, follows the received bit for $\tfrac14 < q < \tfrac78$, and always decides $0$ for $q>\tfrac78$; at $q=\tfrac14$ and $q=\tfrac78$ one output is tied and either choice gives the same error. The minimum error is therefore $$P_{\rm e}(q)=\begin{cases}q,&0\le q\le\tfrac14,\\ \tfrac3{10}-\tfrac q5,&\tfrac14\le q\le\tfrac78,\\ 1-q,&\tfrac78\le q\le1.\end{cases}$$ The first piece increases and the other two decrease, so the maximum is $P_{\rm e}=\tfrac14$ at $q=\tfrac14$.

Option (b) is the equiprobable prior, whose MAP error is only $\tfrac3{10}-\tfrac1{10}=\tfrac15$; option (a) is the other transition point, where the error is $\tfrac18$. For an asymmetric channel the equiprobable prior need not be the hardest one for the optimal detector.
