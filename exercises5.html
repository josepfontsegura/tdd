Exercises: 5. Modulation and demodulation

All waveforms are real, and a complex symbol $x_i$ names the pair of coordinates ${\rm Re}(x_i)$ and ${\rm Im}(x_i)$ on the in-phase and quadrature waveforms $\phi_{{\rm I},i}(t)=\sqrt2\,\psi(t-iT)\cos(2\pi f_{\rm c}t)$ and $\phi_{{\rm Q},i}(t)=-\sqrt2\,\psi(t-iT)\sin(2\pi f_{\rm c}t)$. Pulses have unit energy and are zero outside their stated support, the symbol $x_i$ rides on $\phi(t-iT)$ with $i=1,\ldots,n$, and $g(\tau)=\langle\phi(t),\phi(t-\tau)\rangle$. The rectangular pulse is $\phi(t)=1/\sqrt T$ for $0\leq t\lt T$, and the sinc pulse is $\phi(t)=\tfrac1{\sqrt T}\operatorname{sinc}(t/T)$ with $\operatorname{sinc}(u)=\sin(\pi u)/(\pi u)$. A root raised-cosine pulse of roll-off $\beta$ has bandwidth $W=(1+\beta)/(2T)$, and a passband signal built on a pulse of bandwidth $W$ occupies a passband bandwidth of $2W$. The symbol rate is $R_{\rm s}=1/T$ and the information bit rate is $R_{\rm b}=R\,R_{\rm s}$, with $R=r\log_2M$ for a code of rate $r$, $E_{\rm b}=E_{\rm s}/R$ and $P=E_{\rm s}R_{\rm s}=E_{\rm b}R_{\rm b}$. White Gaussian noise has two-sided spectral density $N_0/2$, so the real channel of PAM has noise variance $N_0/2$ and $\mathrm{SNR}=2E_{\rm s}/N_0$, and the complex channel of QAM has noise variance $N_0$ and $\mathrm{SNR}=E_{\rm s}/N_0$. Constellation points are equiprobable, detection is nearest-point, and Gray labels are used where stated. Answers may be left in terms of $\operatorname{erfc}$, and values quoted from Tables 5.1 and 5.3 of the notes are rounded. Statements that refer to an earlier exercise give its number on this page.

Exercise 5.1.

A transmitter sends uncoded 64-QAM at the symbol rate $R_{\rm s}=5$ Msymbols per second. What is the bit rate $R_{\rm b}$?

(a) $320$ Mbit/s
(b) $30$ Mbit/s
(c) $15$ Mbit/s

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

Without coding each symbol carries $\log_2M=\log_264=6$ bits, so equation (5.1) of Section 5.1 gives $R_{\rm b}=R_{\rm s}\log_2M=5\cdot6=30$ Mbit/s.

Exercise 5.2.

A transmitter carries $R_{\rm b}=2$ Mbit/s with QPSK and a binary code of rate $r=\tfrac12$ before the mapper. What is the symbol period $T$?

(a) $1\,\mu$s
(b) $2\,\mu$s
(c) $0.5\,\mu$s

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

By Section 5.1, each symbol carries $R=r\log_2M=\tfrac12\cdot2=1$ information bit, and $R_{\rm b}=R\,R_{\rm s}$ gives $R_{\rm s}=2$ Msymbols per second, so $T=1/R_{\rm s}=0.5\,\mu$s.

Exercise 5.3.

A transmitter uses 64-QAM with a binary code of rate $r=\tfrac23$ before the mapper, and the energy per symbol is $E_{\rm s}=8$ nJ. What is the energy per information bit $E_{\rm b}$?

(a) $2$ nJ
(b) $\tfrac43$ nJ
(c) $12$ nJ

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

By Section 5.1, each symbol carries $R=r\log_2M=\tfrac23\cdot6=4$ information bits, and $E_{\rm b}=E_{\rm s}/R=8/4=2$ nJ.

Exercise 5.4.

An uncoded 16-QAM link sends $R_{\rm s}=2.5$ Msymbols per second with the energy per bit $E_{\rm b}=2$ pJ. What is the average transmitted power $P$?

(a) $80\,\mu$W
(b) $5\,\mu$W
(c) $20\,\mu$W

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

The bit rate is $R_{\rm b}=4R_{\rm s}=10$ Mbit/s, and equation (5.2) gives $P=E_{\rm b}R_{\rm b}=2\cdot10^{-12}\cdot10^7=20\,\mu$W. The other form of (5.2) agrees: $E_{\rm s}=4E_{\rm b}=8$ pJ and $P=E_{\rm s}R_{\rm s}=8\cdot10^{-12}\cdot2.5\cdot10^6=20\,\mu$W.

Exercise 5.5.

A transmitter keeps the bit rate $R_{\rm b}$ and the energy per bit $E_{\rm b}$ fixed, and replaces uncoded QPSK by uncoded 64-QAM. What happens to the average transmitted power $P$?

(a) It is multiplied by $3$, because $E_{\rm s}$ is three times larger.
(b) It does not change.
(c) It is divided by $3$, because the symbol rate is divided by $3$.

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

By equation (5.2), $P=E_{\rm b}R_{\rm b}$, which does not depend on the constellation. The energy per symbol grows from $2E_{\rm b}$ to $6E_{\rm b}$, but the symbol rate falls from $R_{\rm b}/2$ to $R_{\rm b}/6$, and the product $E_{\rm s}R_{\rm s}$ stays the same.

Exercise 5.6.

An uncoded 8-PAM constellation with the equiprobable points $\pm a,\pm3a,\pm5a,\pm7a$ has the energy per bit $E_{\rm b}=14$ nJ. What is its squared minimum distance $d_{\min}^2$?

(a) $8$ nJ
(b) $\tfrac83$ nJ
(c) $2$ nJ

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

By Table 5.1, 8-PAM has $E_{\rm s}=21a^2$ and carries $3$ bits per symbol, so $E_{\rm b}=7a^2=14$ nJ and $a^2=2$ nJ. Neighbors are $2a$ apart, so $d_{\min}^2=4a^2=8$ nJ, in agreement with $d_{\min}^2/E_{\rm b}=0.57$ in the table.

Exercise 5.7.

An uncoded 16-QAM system with a Gray labeling has the symbol error probability $P_{\rm e}=4\cdot10^{-4}$, at an SNR where the errors to a neighboring point dominate. What is, approximately, its bit error probability $P_{\rm b}$?

(a) $10^{-4}$
(b) $4\cdot10^{-4}$
(c) $1.6\cdot10^{-3}$

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

With a Gray labeling, neighboring points differ in one bit, so an error to a neighbor costs one of the $\log_2M=4$ bits of the symbol. Section 5.1 gives $P_{\rm b}\approx P_{\rm e}/\log_2M=10^{-4}$.

Exercise 5.8.

According to Table 5.1, which two constellations have the same squared minimum distance per unit of energy per bit, $d_{\min}^2/E_{\rm b}$, for uncoded transmission with equiprobable points?

(a) QPSK and 4-PAM
(b) 8-PSK and 16-QAM
(c) QPSK and 2-PAM

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

2-PAM has $E_{\rm b}=a^2$ and $d_{\min}^2=4a^2$. QPSK has $E_{\rm s}=2a^2$ and two bits per symbol, so again $E_{\rm b}=a^2$, with the same $d_{\min}^2=4a^2$. Both give $d_{\min}^2/E_{\rm b}=4$ in Table 5.1: the quadrature coordinate carries a second bit at no cost in energy per bit.

Exercise 5.9.

An uncoded link with equiprobable constellation points replaces 16-QAM by 64-QAM, keeping the symbol rate $R_{\rm s}$ and the spacing $2a$ between neighboring points. By what factor is the transmitted power multiplied?

(a) $2.8$
(b) $4.2$
(c) $1.5$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

By Table 5.1 the energy per symbol grows from $10a^2$ to $42a^2$, and by equation (5.2), $P=E_{\rm s}R_{\rm s}$ with $R_{\rm s}$ fixed, so the power is multiplied by $42/10=4.2$.

Exercise 5.10.

Which condition is necessary and sufficient for a real pulse $\phi$ of unit energy to be a Nyquist pulse for the symbol period $T$?

(a) The pulse is zero outside an interval of length $T$, so that its shifts by multiples of $T$ do not overlap.
(b) $\phi(kT)=0$ for every integer $k\neq0$.
(c) $g(kT)=0$ for every integer $k\neq0$, where $g(\tau)=\langle\phi(t),\phi(t-\tau)\rangle$.

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

By Definition 5.2, $\phi$ is a Nyquist pulse when its shifts $\phi(t-iT)$ are orthonormal, that is, when the function $g$ of (5.3) has $g(0)=1$ and $g(kT)=0$ for $k\neq0$. Unit energy already gives $g(0)=1$, so the condition is $g(kT)=0$ for $k\neq0$.

Exercise 5.11.

The pulse $\phi(t)=1/\sqrt{2T}$ for $0\leq t\lt 2T$, and zero otherwise, has unit energy and is used with the symbol period $T$. Which statement is true?

(a) $g(T)=\tfrac12$, so $\phi$ is not a Nyquist pulse for the period $T$.
(b) $g(T)=0$, so $\phi$ is a Nyquist pulse for the period $T$, like every rectangular pulse of unit energy.
(c) $g(T)=\tfrac12$, but $\phi$ is a Nyquist pulse for the period $T$ because $g(2T)=0$.

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

The pulse and its shift by $T$ overlap on $[T,2T)$, an interval of length $T$ where their product is $\tfrac1{2T}$, so $g(T)=T\cdot\tfrac1{2T}=\tfrac12$. Definition 5.2 requires $g(kT)=0$ for every $k\neq0$, and the condition fails at $k=1$.

Exercise 5.12.

The 4-PAM symbols $(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)=(3a,-a,-3a,a)$ are sent as $x(t)=\sum_{i=1}^4x_i\phi(t-iT)$ with the sinc pulse $\phi(t)=\tfrac1{\sqrt T}\operatorname{sinc}(t/T)$, whose shifts overlap over the whole real line. What is the energy of $x(t)$?

(a) $5a^2$
(b) $20a^2$
(c) $0$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

The shifts of the sinc pulse are orthonormal (Example 5.4), so Proposition 4.4 of Topic 4 applies, as Section 5.2 says: the energy is the sum of the squared coordinates, $9a^2+a^2+9a^2+a^2=20a^2$. The overlap in time does not matter, because the cross terms are inner products of orthogonal shifts and vanish.

Exercise 5.13.

A baseband uncoded 8-PAM system has the energy per bit $E_{\rm b}=2\cdot10^{-20}$ J, and the white Gaussian noise has spectral density $N_0/2$ with $N_0=4\cdot10^{-20}$ J. The receiver correlates with the shifts of a Nyquist pulse. What is the SNR of the real Gaussian channel seen by the detector?

(a) $1.5$
(b) $1$
(c) $3$

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

The energy per symbol is $E_{\rm s}=3E_{\rm b}=6\cdot10^{-20}$ J, and by Section 5.2 each correlator output (5.5) has the noise variance $\sigma^2=N_0/2=2\cdot10^{-20}$ J. Hence $\mathrm{SNR}=E_{\rm s}/\sigma^2=2E_{\rm s}/N_0=3$.

Exercise 5.14.

Two baseband uncoded 2-PAM systems have the same symbol period $T$, the same $E_{\rm b}$ and the same $N_0$. One uses the rectangular pulse $\phi(t)=1/\sqrt T$ for $0\leq t\lt T$, the other the sinc pulse $\phi(t)=\tfrac1{\sqrt T}\operatorname{sinc}(t/T)$, and each receiver correlates with the shifts of its own pulse. Which statement is true?

(a) They have the same bit error probability, and they differ in the band of frequencies they occupy.
(b) The sinc system has the larger bit error probability, because its pulses overlap in time.
(c) The rectangular system has the larger bit error probability, because its spectrum is wider and lets in more noise.

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

Both pulses are Nyquist pulses for the period $T$ (Examples 5.3 and 5.4), so both systems see the real Gaussian channel (5.5) with noise variance $N_0/2$, and the error probability depends only on the coordinates and on $N_0$. As Example 5.4 says, the two pulses differ only in the resources they use: the rectangular pulse is confined in time and spread in frequency, and the sinc pulse is the opposite.

Exercise 5.15.

The symbols $(x_1,x_2,x_3)=(a,-3a,a)$ are sent as $x(t)=\sum_{i=1}^3x_i\phi(t-iT)$ with the sinc pulse $\phi(t)=\tfrac1{\sqrt T}\operatorname{sinc}(t/T)$. What is $x(2T)$?

(a) $-a/\sqrt T$
(b) $-3a/\sqrt T$
(c) $-3a$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

At $t=2T$ the $i$-th pulse equals $\tfrac1{\sqrt T}\operatorname{sinc}(2-i)$, which is $\tfrac1{\sqrt T}$ for $i=2$ and $0$ for $i=1$ and $i=3$, since the sinc function vanishes at the nonzero integers. Hence $x(2T)=x_2/\sqrt T=-3a/\sqrt T$. As Example 5.4 and Figure 5.3 show, the signal passes through $x_i/\sqrt T$ at $t=iT$.

Exercise 5.16.

For the sinc pulse $\phi(t)=\tfrac1{\sqrt T}\operatorname{sinc}(t/T)$, what is the inner product $\langle\phi(t),\phi(t-T/2)\rangle$?

(a) $\tfrac12$
(b) $2/\pi$
(c) $0$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

Section 5.5 shows that the sinc pulse has $g(\tau)=\operatorname{sinc}(\tau/T)$, so the inner product is $g(T/2)=\operatorname{sinc}(\tfrac12)=\sin(\pi/2)/(\pi/2)=2/\pi\approx0.64$.

Exercise 5.17.

Each of the following pulses has unit energy and is zero outside the stated interval. Which one is a Nyquist pulse for the period $T$?

(a) $\phi(t)=\sqrt{2/(3T)}$ for $0\leq t\lt\tfrac32T$
(b) $\phi(t)=\sqrt{3/(2T)}\,(1-|t|/T)$ for $|t|\lt T$
(c) $\phi(t)=1/\sqrt T$ for $0\leq t\lt\tfrac T2$ and $\phi(t)=-1/\sqrt T$ for $\tfrac T2\leq t\lt T$

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

The third pulse lives in $[0,T)$, so its shifts by multiples of $T$ have disjoint supports and are orthonormal, as for the rectangular pulse of Example 5.3. Definition 5.2 is satisfied, although the pulse changes sign in the middle of the period.

Exercise 5.18.

A PAM system uses the unit-energy pulse $\phi(t)=\sqrt{3/T}\;t/T$ for $0\leq t\lt T$, and zero otherwise. Which filter, sampled at $t=iT$, gives the correlation $\langle Y(t),\phi(t-iT)\rangle$ for every received signal $Y(t)$?

(a) $h(t)=-\sqrt{3/T}\;t/T$ for $-T\lt t\leq0$, and zero otherwise
(b) $h(t)=\sqrt{3/T}\;t/T$ for $0\leq t\lt T$, and zero otherwise
(c) $h(t)=\sqrt{3/T}\,(1-t/T)$ for $0\lt t\leq T$, and zero otherwise

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

By Proposition 5.5, the matched filter is the pulse reversed in time, $h(t)=\phi(-t)$, and its output at $iT$ is the correlation with $\phi(t-iT)$. Reversing the ramp gives $\phi(-t)=\sqrt{3/T}\,(-t)/T$ for $0\leq-t\lt T$, that is, for $-T\lt t\leq0$.

Exercise 5.19.

Uncoded 2-PAM with the symbols $\pm a$ is sent with the rectangular pulse $\phi(t)=1/\sqrt T$ for $0\leq t\lt T$, and the receiver samples the matched filter late, at $t=iT+\tau$ with $0\lt\tau\lt T$. Without noise, for which delays are all the decisions correct, whatever the symbols?

(a) $0\lt\tau\lt T$
(b) $0\lt\tau\lt T/4$
(c) $0\lt\tau\lt T/2$

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

By (5.11) and the triangle (5.6), only the symbol $x_i$ and the next one contribute, and the sample is $(1-\tau/T)x_i+(\tau/T)x_{i+1}$, as in Problem 5.6. The worst case is a next symbol of the opposite sign, which leaves $(1-2\tau/T)x_i$. Its sign is that of $x_i$ exactly when $\tau\lt T/2$, and at $\tau=T/2$ the sample is $0$, on the threshold.

Exercise 5.20.

Repeat Exercise 5.19 for uncoded 4-PAM with the points $\pm a,\pm3a$ and the nearest-point thresholds $0$ and $\pm2a$. For which delays are all the noise-free decisions correct, whatever the symbols?

(a) $0\lt\tau\lt T/2$
(b) $0\lt\tau\lt T/6$
(c) $0\lt\tau\lt T/4$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

With $u=\tau/T$ the sample is $(1-u)x_i+u\,x_{i+1}$, as in Exercise 5.19. For $x_i=3a$ followed by $x_{i+1}=-3a$ it is $3a(1-2u)$, which stays above the threshold $2a$ only if $u\lt\tfrac16$. For $x_i=a$ followed by $-3a$ it is $a(1-4u)$, positive if $u\lt\tfrac14$, and for $x_i=a$ followed by $3a$ it is $a(1+2u)$, below $2a$ if $u\lt\tfrac12$. The negative symbols behave symmetrically, so all the decisions are correct exactly when $\tau\lt T/6$.

Exercise 5.21.

A PAM receiver with a unit-energy Nyquist pulse $\phi$ samples its matched filter at $t=iT+\tau$ instead of $t=iT$. The white Gaussian noise has spectral density $N_0/2$. What is the variance of the noise in each sample?

(a) $N_0/2$, the same as at $t=iT$
(b) $g(\tau)N_0/2$, reduced by the same factor as the symbol $x_i$
(c) $N_0$, because the sample collects noise from two symbol periods

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

By Proposition 5.5 the sample is the correlation $\langle Y(t),\phi(t-iT-\tau)\rangle$, and its noise part is the projection of white noise onto a unit-energy waveform, which has variance $N_0/2$ by Proposition 4.10 of Topic 4, whatever the shift. Problem 5.6 makes the same remark: the timing error costs signal and gains nothing.

Exercise 5.22.

A PAM receiver with the sinc pulse $\phi(t)=\tfrac1{\sqrt T}\operatorname{sinc}(t/T)$ samples the matched filter with a timing error $\tau$, $0\lt\tau\lt T$. As $k$ grows, how fast do the interference coefficients $g(kT+\tau)$ of the distant symbols decrease?

(a) Like $1/k^3$
(b) Like $1/k^2$
(c) Like $1/k$

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

For the sinc pulse $g(\tau)=\operatorname{sinc}(\tau/T)$, so $g(kT+\tau)=\sin(\pi k+\pi\tau/T)/(\pi(k+\tau/T))$, whose numerator has the constant magnitude $|\sin(\pi\tau/T)|$. The coefficients decrease like $1/k$, as Section 5.3 states after (5.11), and the interference gathers contributions from many symbols.

Exercise 5.23.

Let $h(t)=\phi(-t)$. Which expression equals the filter output $(y*h)(t)$ for every real pulse $\phi$, every signal $y$ and every instant $t$?

(a) $\langle y(u),\phi(t-u)\rangle$
(b) $\langle y(u),\phi(u-t)\rangle$
(c) $\langle y(u),\phi(u+t)\rangle$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

By the convolution (5.8), $(y*h)(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}y(u)h(t-u)\,\mathrm du$, and $h(t-u)=\phi(u-t)$, so the output is $\langle y(u),\phi(u-t)\rangle$, the correlation with the pulse shifted to the instant $t$. This is Proposition 5.5, equation (5.9).

Exercise 5.24.

Which statement about the intersymbol interference of Section 5.3 is true?

(a) It is present without noise, it depends on the transmitted symbols, and it vanishes when the matched filter of a Nyquist pulse is sampled at $t=iT$.
(b) It is a Gaussian noise, independent of the symbols, whose variance grows with the timing error.
(c) It disappears when $E_{\rm s}/N_0$ is large enough, like the errors caused by the noise.

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

By (5.11) the interference is $\sum_{k\neq i}x_kg((i-k)T+\tau)$, a combination of the other symbols with coefficients fixed by the pulse and the timing error. It is there without noise, it changes with the data, and it vanishes at $\tau=0$ because $g(kT)=0$ for $k\neq0$, the Nyquist condition.

Exercise 5.25.

With the rectangular pulse $\phi(t)=1/\sqrt T$ for $0\leq t\lt T$ and the matched filter $h(t)=\phi(-t)$, the sample of the filter output at $t=iT$ depends on the received waveform $Y(t)$ over which interval?

(a) $[(i-1)T,iT)$
(b) $[iT-T/2,iT+T/2)$
(c) $[iT,(i+1)T)$

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

By Proposition 5.5, this sample is $\langle Y(t),\phi(t-iT)\rangle$, and $\phi(t-iT)$ is nonzero only on $[iT,(i+1)T)$, the interval of the symbol $x_i$ in Figure 5.2. The filter $\phi(-t)$ is nonzero for $-T\lt t\leq0$, so its output at $iT$ uses the input up to the instant $(i+1)T$.

Exercise 5.26.

A QAM system places the real and imaginary parts of the symbol $x_i$ on the waveforms $\phi_{{\rm I},i}(t)=\sqrt2\,\psi(t-iT)\cos(2\pi f_{\rm c}t)$ and $\phi_{{\rm Q},i}(t)=-\sqrt2\,\psi(t-iT)\sin(2\pi f_{\rm c}t)$, built on a real Nyquist pulse $\psi$. Which is the $i$-th term of the signal for $x_i=-a+ja\sqrt3$, with $a\gt0$?

(a) $2\sqrt2\,a\,\psi(t-iT)\cos(2\pi f_{\rm c}t+2\pi/3)$
(b) $2\sqrt2\,a\,\psi(t-iT)\cos(2\pi f_{\rm c}t-2\pi/3)$
(c) $2a\,\psi(t-iT)\cos(2\pi f_{\rm c}t+2\pi/3)$

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

The symbol has modulus $|x_i|=\sqrt{a^2+3a^2}=2a$ and argument $\theta_i=2\pi/3$, since $\cos\theta_i=-\tfrac12$ and $\sin\theta_i=\tfrac{\sqrt3}2$. By (5.14), the term ${\rm Re}(x_i)\phi_{{\rm I},i}(t)+{\rm Im}(x_i)\phi_{{\rm Q},i}(t)$ equals $\sqrt2\,|x_i|\,\psi(t-iT)\cos(2\pi f_{\rm c}t+\theta_i)=2\sqrt2\,a\,\psi(t-iT)\cos(2\pi f_{\rm c}t+2\pi/3)$.

Exercise 5.27.

A QAM signal uses the waveforms $\phi_{{\rm I},i}(t)=\sqrt2\,\psi(t-iT)\cos(2\pi f_{\rm c}t)$ and $\phi_{{\rm Q},i}(t)=-\sqrt2\,\psi(t-iT)\sin(2\pi f_{\rm c}t)$. Its $i$-th term is $3\sqrt2\,\psi(t-iT)\sin(2\pi f_{\rm c}t)$. Which symbol $x_i$ does it carry?

(a) $3$
(b) $-3j$
(c) $3j$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

The term equals $-3\,\phi_{{\rm Q},i}(t)$, so by (5.13) ${\rm Re}(x_i)=0$ and ${\rm Im}(x_i)=-3$, that is, $x_i=-3j$. The amplitude and phase form (5.14) agrees: $\sin(2\pi f_{\rm c}t)=\cos(2\pi f_{\rm c}t-\pi/2)$, so $|x_i|=3$ and $\theta_i=-\pi/2$.

Exercise 5.28.

A passband QPSK system has the energy per symbol $E_{\rm s}=2\cdot10^{-20}$ J, and the white Gaussian noise has $N_0=5\cdot10^{-21}$ J. What is the SNR of the complex Gaussian channel (5.15) seen by the detector?

(a) $8$
(b) $2$
(c) $4$

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

By Section 5.4, the real and imaginary parts of the complex noise $Z_i$ have variance $N_0/2$ each, so its variance is $\sigma^2=N_0$ and $\mathrm{SNR}=E_{\rm s}/N_0=4$. Each real coordinate has the same ratio, $(E_{\rm s}/2)/(N_0/2)=4$.

Exercise 5.29.

Uncoded QPSK with the points $a(\pm1\pm j)$, $a\gt0$, and a Gray labeling is received in white Gaussian noise with $N_0=a^2/2$ by the nearest-point detector. What is its bit error probability?

(a) $\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}(1)$
(b) $\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt2)$
(c) $\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt2)-\tfrac14\operatorname{erfc}^2(\sqrt2)$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

By Example 5.7, each coordinate is 2-PAM with amplitude $a$ in noise of variance $N_0/2$, wrong with probability $p=\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}(a/\sqrt{N_0})=\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt2)$, and with Gray labels the bit error probability is exactly $p$. Equivalently, $E_{\rm b}=a^2$, so $E_{\rm b}/N_0=2$ and (5.16) gives $\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt2)$.

Exercise 5.30.

Baseband 2-PAM and passband QPSK, both uncoded with Gray labels, use the same symbol period $T$ and operate at the same $E_{\rm b}/N_0$. Which statement is true?

(a) They have the same bit error probability, and QPSK carries twice as many bits per symbol period.
(b) QPSK reaches the same bit error probability only with $3$ dB more $E_{\rm b}/N_0$, because its energy is split between two coordinates.
(c) QPSK has the smaller bit error probability, because its energy per symbol is twice that of 2-PAM.

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

By Example 5.7, the bit error probability of QPSK is $\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{E_{\rm b}/N_0})$, equation (5.16), the same as that of 2-PAM, while each QPSK symbol carries two bits instead of one. The quadrature waveform carries the second bit at no cost in energy per bit.

Exercise 5.31.

A QAM signal carries the three symbols $x_1=a(1+j)$, $x_2=-3ja$ and $x_3=2a$ on orthonormal in-phase and quadrature waveforms. What is its energy?

(a) $30a^2$
(b) $7.5a^2$
(c) $15a^2$

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

The signal (5.13) has the six real coordinates $(a,a,0,-3a,2a,0)$ on an orthonormal basis, so by Proposition 4.4 of Topic 4 its energy is $\sum_i|x_i|^2=2a^2+9a^2+4a^2=15a^2$, as Section 5.4 states.

Exercise 5.32.

16-QAM, with the points $a(u+jv)$ for $u,v\in\{\pm1,\pm3\}$, is sent with the rectangular pulse. By (5.14), the amplitude of the carrier in each symbol period is proportional to $|x_i|$. What is the ratio between the largest and the smallest amplitude?

(a) $9$
(b) $3$
(c) $\sqrt5$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

The largest modulus is that of a corner, $|3a+3ja|=3\sqrt2\,a$, and the smallest that of an inner point, $|a+ja|=\sqrt2\,a$, so the ratio is $3$. Unlike PSK, QAM modulates the amplitude of the carrier as well as its phase.

Exercise 5.33.

A passband system sends $R_{\rm s}=2$ Msymbols per second over the complex Gaussian channel with $E_{\rm s}/N_0=15$. By the remark that closes Section 5.4, which applies Theorem 3.14 of Topic 3, at most what bit rate can be sent reliably?

(a) $8$ Mbit/s
(b) $4$ Mbit/s
(c) $16$ Mbit/s

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

The passband channel is the complex Gaussian channel with $\mathrm{SNR}=E_{\rm s}/N_0$, so at most $\log_2(1+15)=4$ bits per symbol can be sent reliably, that is, at most $R_{\rm s}\log_2(1+E_{\rm s}/N_0)=2\cdot4=8$ Mbit/s. Rates below this limit are reliable with long enough codes.

Exercise 5.34.

A real baseband pulse $\psi$ has a Fourier transform that vanishes for $|f|\gt3$ MHz, and for no smaller limit. What is its bandwidth $W$, and what passband bandwidth does a QAM signal built on $\psi$ with the carrier $f_{\rm c}=100$ MHz occupy?

(a) $W=3$ MHz, and a passband bandwidth of $6$ MHz
(b) $W=6$ MHz, and a passband bandwidth of $6$ MHz
(c) $W=3$ MHz, and a passband bandwidth of $3$ MHz

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

By Definition 5.10, the bandwidth of a baseband signal is the extent of its positive frequencies, $W=3$ MHz, since for a real signal the negative frequencies repeat the positive ones (Table 5.2). By the modulation property, the QAM signal occupies $f_{\rm c}-W\leq|f|\leq f_{\rm c}+W$, from $97$ to $103$ MHz, a passband bandwidth of $2W=6$ MHz.

Exercise 5.35.

What is the energy density $|\hat\phi(f)|^2$ of the rectangular pulse $\phi(t)=1/\sqrt T$ for $0\leq t\lt T$, and zero otherwise?

(a) $\tfrac1T\operatorname{sinc}^2(fT)$
(b) $T\operatorname{sinc}^2(fT)\cos^2(\pi fT)$
(c) $T\operatorname{sinc}^2(fT)$

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

By Example 5.9, the same pulse centered at the origin has the transform $\sqrt T\operatorname{sinc}(fT)$. This pulse is that one shifted by $T/2$, and by the shift property of Table 5.2 the transform is multiplied by $e^{-j\pi fT}$, of modulus one, so $|\hat\phi(f)|^2=T\operatorname{sinc}^2(fT)$.

Exercise 5.36.

A PAM system uses the rectangular pulse $\phi(t)=1/\sqrt T$ for $0\leq t\lt T$, with $T=0.5\,\mu$s. About what fraction of the energy of the pulse lies at the frequencies $|f|\geq2$ MHz?

(a) None, because $\hat\phi(f)$ vanishes at every nonzero multiple of $1/T$
(b) About 10%
(c) About 5%

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

Here $1/T=2$ MHz. By Example 5.9, about 90% of the energy lies in $|f|\lt1/T$, so about 10% lies at $|f|\geq2$ MHz. The energy density $T\operatorname{sinc}^2(fT)$ has zeros at the nonzero multiples of $1/T$ but is positive between them, and it decays only like $1/f^2$.

Exercise 5.37.

A passband channel offers a passband bandwidth of $6$ MHz, the total width of the band around the carrier. With root raised-cosine pulses of roll-off $\beta=0.5$, whose bandwidth is $W=(1+\beta)/(2T)$, what is the largest symbol rate $R_{\rm s}$?

(a) $8$ Msymbols per second
(b) $6$ Msymbols per second
(c) $4$ Msymbols per second

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

By Definition 5.10, the QAM signal occupies the passband bandwidth $2W=(1+\beta)R_{\rm s}$, the value of $W_{\rm total}$ in Section 5.6. The condition $1.5R_{\rm s}\leq6$ MHz gives $R_{\rm s}\leq4$ Msymbols per second.

Exercise 5.38.

A designer proposes a Nyquist pulse for the symbol period $T=1\,\mu$s with a bandwidth of $400$ kHz. Is this possible?

(a) No, a Nyquist pulse for this period has a bandwidth of at least $500$ kHz.
(b) Yes, with a raised-cosine pulse of small enough roll-off $\beta$.
(c) No, a Nyquist pulse for this period has a bandwidth of at least $1$ MHz.

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

By Theorem 5.11, the Nyquist criterion, a Nyquist pulse for the period $T$ has bandwidth at least $1/(2T)=500$ kHz, and the sinc pulse attains it. With a smaller bandwidth the shifted copies in (5.20) would leave gaps between them and could not add up to $T$.

Exercise 5.39.

A baseband PAM signal uses root raised-cosine pulses at the symbol rate $R_{\rm s}=2$ Msymbols per second, and its bandwidth is $W=1.5$ MHz. What is the roll-off $\beta$?

(a) $0.25$
(b) $0.5$
(c) $\tfrac13$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

By Example 5.12, $W=(1+\beta)/(2T)=(1+\beta)R_{\rm s}/2$, so $1.5=(1+\beta)\cdot1$ and $\beta=0.5$. The excess over the minimum $1/(2T)=1$ MHz is $0.5$ MHz, half of the minimum.

Exercise 5.40.

The raised-cosine family has $g(\tau)=\operatorname{sinc}(\tau/T)\,\dfrac{\cos(\pi\beta\tau/T)}{1-(2\beta\tau/T)^2}$. For $\beta=1$, what is $g(3T/2)$?

(a) $-\tfrac2{3\pi}$
(b) $\tfrac1{12\pi}$
(c) $0$

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

At $\tau=3T/2$ with $\beta=1$, (5.23) has $\operatorname{sinc}(\tfrac32)=-\tfrac2{3\pi}$, the cosine $\cos(3\pi/2)=0$ and the denominator $1-9=-8$, which is not zero. Hence $g(3T/2)=0$. With $\beta=1$ the cosine vanishes at every odd multiple of $T/2$, so $g$ is zero at every nonzero multiple of $T/2$ except $\pm T/2$, where the quotient has a finite nonzero limit.

Exercise 5.41.

At a fixed symbol period $T$, the roll-off of a root raised-cosine pulse is increased from $\beta=0.2$ to $\beta=0.5$. Which statement is true?

(a) The bandwidth is unchanged, since it is set by $1/(2T)$, and only the decay of $g$ changes.
(b) The bandwidth is multiplied by $1.25$, and the envelope of the tails of $g$ becomes smaller.
(c) The bandwidth is multiplied by $2.5$, and the envelope of the tails of $g$ becomes larger.

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

By Example 5.12 the bandwidth is $(1+\beta)/(2T)$, which grows from $1.2/(2T)$ to $1.5/(2T)$, a factor $1.25$. Figure 5.5 shows the other side of the exchange. For every $\beta\gt0$ the decay is like $1/\tau^3$ (Example 5.12), and by (5.23) the envelope of the tails is proportional to $1/\beta^2$ for large $\tau$, so a larger $\beta$ makes it smaller.

Exercise 5.42.

A real pulse of unit energy has the energy density $|\hat\phi(f)|^2=T/2$ for $|f|\lt1/T$, and $0$ otherwise. Is it a Nyquist pulse for the period $T$?

(a) Yes, because its copies shifted by the multiples of $1/T$ add up to $T$ at almost every frequency.
(b) No, because its energy density on the band is $T/2$ and not $T$.
(c) No, because its bandwidth $1/T$ is larger than the minimum $1/(2T)$.

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

Each copy $|\hat\phi(f-k/T)|^2$ covers an interval of width $2/T$, and the copies are $1/T$ apart, so almost every frequency lies in exactly two of them and the sum (5.20) is $2\cdot T/2=T$. By Theorem 5.11 the pulse is a Nyquist pulse for the period $T$. Its $g$, the inverse transform (5.19), is $\operatorname{sinc}(2\tau/T)$, which vanishes at every nonzero multiple of $T/2$.

Exercise 5.43.

The sinc pulse $\phi(t)=\tfrac1{\sqrt T}\operatorname{sinc}(t/T)$ is used with $T=1\,\mu$s. What is its energy density $|\hat\phi(f)|^2$, in seconds, at $f=200$ kHz and at $f=600$ kHz?

(a) $10^{-3}$ and $0$
(b) $10^{-6}$ and $10^{-6}$
(c) $10^{-6}$ and $0$

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

By the last line of Table 5.2, $\hat\phi(f)=\sqrt T$ for $|f|\lt1/(2T)=500$ kHz and $0$ beyond, so $|\hat\phi(f)|^2=T=10^{-6}$ at $200$ kHz and $0$ at $600$ kHz. The rectangle of height $T$ and width $1/T$ has area $1$, the energy of the pulse.

Exercise 5.44.

A QAM signal built on the sinc pulse occupies exactly the frequencies $1990\leq|f|\leq2010$ MHz. What is its symbol rate $R_{\rm s}$?

(a) $40$ Msymbols per second
(b) $20$ Msymbols per second
(c) $10$ Msymbols per second

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

By Definition 5.10, the passband bandwidth is $2W=20$ MHz, so the sinc pulse has $W=10$ MHz, which is $1/(2T)$, and $R_{\rm s}=1/T=20$ Msymbols per second. This is the equality case of (5.21): in passband, $R_{\rm s}\leq2W$ complex symbols per second in a passband bandwidth of $2W$.

Exercise 5.45.

Uncoded baseband 8-PAM uses root raised-cosine pulses with roll-off $\beta=0.5$. What is its spectral efficiency $\eta$, in bits per second per hertz?

(a) $4$
(b) $2$
(c) $6$

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

By (5.25), baseband PAM has $\eta_{\rm PAM}=2R/(1+\beta)$, and uncoded 8-PAM carries $R=3$ bits per symbol, so $\eta=6/1.5=4$. Directly, $R_{\rm b}=3R_{\rm s}$ in the bandwidth $W_{\rm total}=1.5R_{\rm s}/2$.

Exercise 5.46.

Passband 64-QAM with a binary code of rate $r=\tfrac23$ before the mapper uses root raised-cosine pulses with roll-off $\beta=0.25$. What is its spectral efficiency $\eta$, in bits per second per hertz?

(a) $4.8$
(b) $6.4$
(c) $3.2$

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

Each symbol carries $R=r\log_2M=\tfrac23\cdot6=4$ information bits, and (5.25) gives $\eta_{\rm QAM}=R/(1+\beta)=4/1.25=3.2$. The code reduces $\eta$ by the factor $r$, as Section 5.6 says.

Exercise 5.47.

Uncoded baseband 4-PAM and uncoded passband 16-QAM, both with equiprobable constellation points, use root raised-cosine pulses with the same roll-off and occupy the same total bandwidth $W_{\rm total}$. Which statement is true?

(a) They have the same spectral efficiency and the same $d_{\min}^2/E_{\rm b}$.
(b) 4-PAM has twice the spectral efficiency, because of the factor $2$ in $\eta_{\rm PAM}=2R/(1+\beta)$.
(c) 16-QAM has twice the spectral efficiency, because it carries $4$ bits per symbol instead of $2$.

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

By (5.25), 4-PAM has $\eta=2\cdot2/(1+\beta)$ and 16-QAM has $\eta=4/(1+\beta)$, the same value. Both use the same $2W_{\rm total}/(1+\beta)$ real dimensions per second with $2$ bits on each, and Table 5.1 gives both $d_{\min}^2/E_{\rm b}=1.6$, since each coordinate of 16-QAM is a 4-PAM.

Exercise 5.48.

Uncoded QAM with root raised-cosine pulses of roll-off $\beta=0.25$ switches from 16-QAM to 64-QAM, for a target bit error probability of $10^{-3}$. Table 5.3 gives the required $E_{\rm b}/N_0$ as $10.5$ dB for 16-QAM and $14.8$ dB for 64-QAM. How much do the spectral efficiency and the required $E_{\rm b}/N_0$ increase?

(a) $\eta$ increases by $2$ bits per second per hertz and $E_{\rm b}/N_0$ by $4.3$ dB.
(b) $\eta$ increases by $1.6$ bits per second per hertz and $E_{\rm b}/N_0$ by $4.3$ dB.
(c) $\eta$ increases by $1.6$ bits per second per hertz and $E_{\rm b}/N_0$ by $6.2$ dB.

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

By (5.25), $\eta$ grows from $4/1.25=3.2$ to $6/1.25=4.8$, that is, by $2/(1+\beta)=1.6$ bits per second per hertz, and the required $E_{\rm b}/N_0$ grows by $14.8-10.5=4.3$ dB. This is the exchange that Section 5.6 describes, about $4$ dB of energy per bit for each additional bit per real dimension.

Uncoded QPSK must reach $P_{\rm b}=10^{-3}$ at $R_{\rm b}=10$ Mbit/s, with $N_0=10^{-20}$ J at the receiver. Table 5.3 gives $E_{\rm b}/N_0=6.8$ dB, that is, about $4.8$. What received power is needed?

Exercise 5.50.

An uncoded 16-QAM system is modified by adding a binary code of rate $r=\tfrac34$ before the mapper, keeping the same constellation, symbol rate and pulse. Which statement is true?

(a) The spectral efficiency is unchanged, because the symbol rate and the bandwidth are unchanged.
(b) The spectral efficiency is multiplied by $\tfrac34$, and a good code can reduce the $E_{\rm b}/N_0$ required for the same $P_{\rm b}$.
(c) The spectral efficiency is multiplied by $\tfrac43$, because the code adds bits to every symbol.

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

With the code, each symbol carries $R=\tfrac34\cdot4=3$ information bits instead of $4$, so at the same symbol rate and bandwidth the information bit rate, and with it $\eta=R_{\rm b}/W_{\rm total}$, is multiplied by $\tfrac34$. Section 5.6 states both sides of the exchange: $R=r\log_2M$ reduces $\eta$ by the factor $r$, and a good code reduces the $E_{\rm b}/N_0$ needed for the same bit error probability, within the limit set by Theorem 3.14 of Topic 3.
