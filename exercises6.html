Exercises: 6. Continuous-channel capacity

The bandwidth $W$ is the band that the signal occupies, baseband or passband, $P$ is the received power and the noise is white and Gaussian with spectral density $N_0/2$, so $\mathrm{SNR}=P/(N_0W)$. Reliable transmission means an error probability as small as desired. The spectral efficiency is $\eta=R_{\rm b}/W$, in bits per second per hertz, and $P=E_{\rm b}R_{\rm b}$. Parallel channels are $Y_k=h_kX_k+Z_k$ with independent complex Gaussian noises of variance $\sigma^2$, energies $E_k$ and total energy $E_{\rm s}$ per use of the set, and rates use independent Gaussian inputs. A link with several antennas has the gain matrix $H$, total energy $E_{\rm s}$ per channel use and $\mathrm{SNR}=E_{\rm s}/\sigma^2$, both ends know $H$, and its spatial modes have the gains $\lambda_k$. For hand calculations, $\log_23=1.585$, $\log_25=2.322$, $\log_27=2.807$, $\log_29=3.170$, $\log_211=3.459$, $\log_213=3.700$, $\log_2e=1.443$ and $\ln2=0.693$, and the ratios $1.25$, $1.6$, $2$, $2.5$, $3.2$, $4$, $5$ and $8$ are $1$, $2$, $3$, $4$, $5$, $6$, $7$ and $9$ dB, and $\tfrac73$ and $3.75$ are $3.7$ and $5.7$ dB. Values quoted from Table 6.1 of the notes and from Table 5.3 of Topic 5 are rounded. Each statement is self-contained.

Exercise 6.1.

A passband channel has $W=10$ MHz and $N_0=4\cdot10^{-21}$ J, and the received power is $P=2.8\cdot10^{-13}$ W. What is the signal-to-noise ratio?

(a) $14$
(b) $7$
(c) $\tfrac72$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

The noise power in the band is $N_0W=4\cdot10^{-14}$ W, so equation (6.1) gives $\mathrm{SNR}=P/(N_0W)=2.8\cdot10^{-13}/(4\cdot10^{-14})=7$.

Exercise 6.2.

A channel has $W=5$ MHz and $\mathrm{SNR}=31$. What is its capacity?

(a) $25$ Mbit/s
(b) $\tfrac{25}2$ Mbit/s
(c) $155$ Mbit/s

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

By Theorem 6.1, $C=W\log_2(1+\mathrm{SNR})=5\cdot10^6\cdot\log_232=25$ Mbit/s.

Exercise 6.3.

The capacity of a Gaussian waveform channel is $C$ bits per second. Which statement is true?

(a) $C$ is the bit rate of uncoded QAM with the sinc pulse at $P_{\rm b}=10^{-5}$.
(b) At bit rates below $C$ the error probability is exactly zero, and above $C$ it is $\tfrac12$.
(c) For every bit rate $R_{\rm b}\lt C$ there are codes and waveforms with an error probability as small as desired, and for $R_{\rm b}\gt C$ there are none.

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

This is the channel coding theorem, Theorem 3.9 of Topic 3, read in seconds after Theorem 6.1. The capacity separates the bit rates at which the error probability can be made as small as desired from those at which it cannot, whatever the modulation and the code.

Exercise 6.4.

A passband signal occupies $W=8$ MHz at the largest symbol rate, $R_{\rm s}=W$, and is received with power $P=4\cdot10^{-13}$ W. What is the energy per complex symbol?

(a) $5\cdot10^{-20}$ J
(b) $2.5\cdot10^{-20}$ J
(c) $10^{-19}$ J

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

By equation (5.2) of Topic 5, as used in Section 6.1, $E_{\rm s}=P/R_{\rm s}=P/W=4\cdot10^{-13}/(8\cdot10^6)=5\cdot10^{-20}$ J.

Exercise 6.5.

What is the smallest signal-to-noise ratio above which $30$ Mbit/s can be sent reliably in a bandwidth of $10$ MHz?

(a) $3$
(b) $8$
(c) $7$

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

Theorem 6.1 requires $R_{\rm b}\lt C$, that is, $3\lt\log_2(1+\mathrm{SNR})$, so $\mathrm{SNR}\gt2^3-1=7$, as in Example 6.2.

Exercise 6.6.

A channel has $W=1$ MHz and $N_0=4\cdot10^{-21}$ J. What is the smallest received power above which $4$ Mbit/s can be sent reliably?

(a) $6\cdot10^{-14}$ W
(b) $6.4\cdot10^{-14}$ W
(c) $1.6\cdot10^{-14}$ W

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

The bit rate needs $4$ bits per second per hertz, so Theorem 6.1 requires $\mathrm{SNR}\gt2^4-1=15$. The noise power in the band is $N_0W=4\cdot10^{-15}$ W, and by equation (6.1) the power must exceed $15\cdot4\cdot10^{-15}=6\cdot10^{-14}$ W.

Exercise 6.7.

A baseband channel and a passband channel have the same bandwidth $W$, the same received power $P$ and the same $N_0$. How do their capacities compare?

(a) The passband channel has twice the capacity, because each of its symbols is complex.
(b) They are equal.
(c) The baseband channel has twice the capacity, because it carries $2W$ symbols per second.

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

By Section 6.1 both bands offer $2W$ real dimensions per second, either as $2W$ real symbols or as $W$ complex ones, and in both cases $\mathrm{SNR}=P/(N_0W)$. Theorem 6.1 gives the same capacity, $W\log_2(1+\mathrm{SNR})$.

Exercise 6.8.

A channel has $W=2$ MHz and $P/N_0=6\cdot10^6$ s$^{-1}$. What is its capacity?

(a) $2\log_23$ Mbit/s
(b) $2$ Mbit/s
(c) $4$ Mbit/s

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

By equation (6.1), $\mathrm{SNR}=P/(N_0W)=6\cdot10^6/(2\cdot10^6)=3$, and by Theorem 6.1, $C=2\cdot10^6\cdot\log_24=4$ Mbit/s.

Exercise 6.9.

The bandwidth of a channel is doubled and the power is raised so that the signal-to-noise ratio stays the same. What happens to the capacity?

(a) It doubles.
(b) It grows, but it does not double.
(c) It does not change.

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

By Theorem 6.1, $C=W\log_2(1+\mathrm{SNR})$. At a fixed SNR the capacity is proportional to the bandwidth, as Section 6.2 remarks, so it doubles.

Exercise 6.10.

A channel has $W=1$ MHz and $P/N_0=7\cdot10^6$ s$^{-1}$, so its capacity is $3$ Mbit/s. What is the capacity with twice the power and the same bandwidth?

(a) $6$ Mbit/s
(b) $4$ Mbit/s
(c) $\log_215$ Mbit/s

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

The noise power does not change, so $\mathrm{SNR}$ goes from $7$ to $14$ and Theorem 6.1 gives $C=10^6\cdot\log_215=3.91$ Mbit/s. This uses the same calculation as Problem 6.3, with $\log_215=\log_23+\log_25=3.91$.

Exercise 6.11.

A channel has $W=1$ MHz and $P/N_0=7\cdot10^6$ s$^{-1}$. What is the capacity with twice the bandwidth and the same power?

(a) $2\log_2\tfrac92$ Mbit/s
(b) $6$ Mbit/s
(c) $2\log_2\tfrac72$ Mbit/s

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

With twice the bandwidth the noise power doubles, so $\mathrm{SNR}=3.5$ and Theorem 6.1 gives $C=2\cdot10^6\cdot\log_24.5=2\cdot10^6\,(\log_29-1)=4.34$ Mbit/s, as in Problem 6.3.

Exercise 6.12.

A channel has $P/N_0=5\cdot10^6$ s$^{-1}$ and may use as much bandwidth as desired. What is the largest bit rate at which reliable transmission is possible?

(a) $5$ Mbit/s
(b) It grows without bound with the bandwidth
(c) $5\log_2e$ Mbit/s

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

By equation (6.6), the capacity with unlimited bandwidth is $C_\infty=\tfrac{P}{N_0}\log_2e=1.44\cdot5\cdot10^6=7.2\cdot10^6$ bits per second. The capacity grows with the bandwidth and never exceeds this value.

Exercise 6.13.

A channel works at a very high signal-to-noise ratio. What does twice the power add to its capacity, approximately?

(a) Nothing, because the bandwidth limits the transmission.
(b) It doubles the capacity.
(c) One bit per second per hertz, that is, $W$ bits per second.

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

In the bandwidth-limited regime, equation (6.4) gives $C\approx W\log_2\mathrm{SNR}$, and $\log_2(2\,\mathrm{SNR})=1+\log_2\mathrm{SNR}$. Twice the power adds one bit per second per hertz, that is, $W$ bits per second.

Exercise 6.14.

A channel has $W=100$ MHz and $\mathrm{SNR}=0.01$. What is its capacity, approximately?

(a) $1$ Mbit/s
(b) $\log_2e$ Mbit/s
(c) $\tfrac12\log_2e$ Mbit/s

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

Here $P/N_0=\mathrm{SNR}\cdot W=10^6$ s$^{-1}$, and at this low SNR equation (6.5) gives $C\approx\tfrac{P}{N_0}\log_2e=1.44\cdot10^6$ bits per second.

Exercise 6.15.

A channel works at a very low signal-to-noise ratio. Its bandwidth is doubled and the power stays the same. What happens to the capacity?

(a) It hardly changes.
(b) It almost doubles.
(c) It is almost halved.

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

In the power-limited regime, equation (6.5) gives $C\approx\tfrac{P}{N_0}\log_2e$, which does not depend on the bandwidth. The capacity grows slightly and stays below $C_\infty$, as Figure 6.1 shows.

Exercise 6.16.

A channel has the bandwidth $W=P/N_0$, the dot of Figure 6.1. What is its capacity, compared with the capacity $C_\infty$ of an unlimited bandwidth?

(a) $C_\infty$
(b) $P/N_0$ bits per second, the fraction $\ln2$ of $C_\infty$
(c) Half of $C_\infty$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

With $W=P/N_0$ equation (6.1) gives $\mathrm{SNR}=1$, so Theorem 6.1 gives $C=W\log_22=P/N_0$ bits per second. By equation (6.6), $C_\infty=1.44\,P/N_0$, so the ratio is $1/1.44=0.69$.

Exercise 6.17.

A channel has $W=1$ MHz and $\mathrm{SNR}=63$, so its capacity is $6$ Mbit/s. What are the capacities with twice the power and with twice the bandwidth at the original power?

(a) $12$ Mbit/s and $2\log_2\tfrac{65}2$ Mbit/s
(b) $\log_2127$ Mbit/s and $12$ Mbit/s
(c) $\log_2127$ Mbit/s and $2\log_2\tfrac{65}2$ Mbit/s

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

With twice the power $\mathrm{SNR}=126$ and $C=10^6\cdot\log_2127=6.99$ Mbit/s. With twice the bandwidth the noise power doubles, so $\mathrm{SNR}=31.5$ and $C=2\cdot10^6\cdot\log_2\tfrac{65}2=10.04$ Mbit/s. At high SNR the bandwidth is worth much more than the power, as Problem 6.3 remarks.

Exercise 6.18.

A system sends $6$ Mbit/s in a bandwidth of $2$ MHz with $E_{\rm b}/N_0=4$. What is the signal-to-noise ratio in the band?

(a) $\tfrac43$
(b) $12$
(c) $4$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

The spectral efficiency is $\eta=R_{\rm b}/W=3$ bits per second per hertz, and equation (6.9) gives $\mathrm{SNR}=\eta\,E_{\rm b}/N_0=3\cdot4=12$.

Exercise 6.19.

What is the smallest $E_{\rm b}/N_0$ at which reliable transmission is possible at $\eta=1$ bit per second per hertz?

(a) $1$
(b) $\ln2$
(c) $2$

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

By Proposition 6.4, $E_{\rm b}/N_0\gt(2^\eta-1)/\eta=(2-1)/1=1$, that is, $0$ dB.

Exercise 6.20.

What is the smallest $E_{\rm b}/N_0$ at which reliable transmission is possible at $\eta=3$ bits per second per hertz?

(a) $7$
(b) $\tfrac83$
(c) $\tfrac73$

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

By Proposition 6.4, $E_{\rm b}/N_0\gt(2^\eta-1)/\eta=(2^3-1)/3=2.33$, that is, $3.7$ dB, the value of Table 6.1.

Exercise 6.21.

What is the Shannon limit, the smallest $E_{\rm b}/N_0$ at which reliable transmission is possible with unlimited bandwidth?

(a) $\ln2$
(b) $1$
(c) $1/\ln2$

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

By Proposition 6.4, the bound $(2^\eta-1)/\eta$ increases with $\eta$, so it is smallest as $\eta$ tends to zero, where it tends to $\ln2=0.693$, that is, $-1.59$ dB.

Exercise 6.22.

Can a system of spectral efficiency $\eta=4$ work reliably at $E_{\rm b}/N_0=3$?

(a) Yes, because $3$ is above the Shannon limit $\ln2$.
(b) No, because the bound at $\eta=4$ is $\tfrac{15}4$.
(c) Yes, with a sufficiently long code at any efficiency.

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

By Proposition 6.4 and Table 6.1, at $\eta=4$ reliable transmission needs $E_{\rm b}/N_0\gt(2^4-1)/4=3.75$, that is, $5.7$ dB. The point at $3$ is to the left of the curve of Figure 6.2. The Shannon limit $\ln2$ holds only at zero efficiency.

Exercise 6.23.

Uncoded 16-QAM with the sinc pulse has $\eta=4$ and needs $E_{\rm b}/N_0=13.4$ dB for $P_{\rm b}=10^{-5}$. How far is it from the bound at its spectral efficiency?

(a) $13.4-10\log_{10}\ln2$ dB
(b) $13.4$ dB
(c) $13.4-10\log_{10}\tfrac{15}4$ dB

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

By Table 6.1, the bound at $\eta=4$ is $5.7$ dB, $10\log_{10}\tfrac{15}4=5.7$ dB, so the distance is $13.4-10\log_{10}\tfrac{15}4=13.4-5.7=7.7$ dB, as in Example 6.5.

Exercise 6.24.

QPSK with the sinc pulse needs $E_{\rm b}/N_0=9.6$ dB for $P_{\rm b}=10^{-5}$ at $\eta=2$, at $7.8$ dB from the bound. A repetition code with $n=2$ and soft decisions is added, which needs the same $E_{\rm b}/N_0$. Where is the new system?

(a) At $\eta=1$, and $9.6$ dB from the bound.
(b) At $\eta=1$, and still $7.8$ dB from the bound.
(c) At $\eta=2$, and $4.8$ dB from the bound.

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

By Example 6.5, the repetition code needs the same $E_{\rm b}/N_0$ and divides the spectral efficiency by $n$, so the system is at $\eta=1$ and $9.6$ dB. The bound at $\eta=1$ is $0$ dB by Table 6.1, so the distance grows to $9.6$ dB.

Exercise 6.25.

A receiver has $N_0=4\cdot10^{-21}$ J. What is the smallest energy per information bit with which reliable transmission is possible, whatever the bandwidth and the code?

(a) $4\cdot10^{-21}$ J
(b) $4\ln2\cdot10^{-21}$ J
(c) $\tfrac4{\ln2}\cdot10^{-21}$ J

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

By the Shannon limit of Section 6.3, $E_{\rm b}\gt N_0\ln2=0.693\cdot4\cdot10^{-21}=2.77\cdot10^{-21}$ J.

Exercise 6.26.

A deep-space probe is received with $P/N_0=1000$ s$^{-1}$ and may use any bandwidth. What is the largest bit rate at which reliable transmission is possible?

(a) $1000\ln2$ bits per second
(b) $1000$ bits per second
(c) $1000\log_2e$ bits per second

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

The Shannon limit $E_{\rm b}/N_0\gt\ln2$ with $P=E_{\rm b}R_{\rm b}$ gives $R_{\rm b}\lt\tfrac{P}{N_0\ln2}=1.44\cdot1000=1440$ bits per second, which is $C_\infty$ of equation (6.6).

Exercise 6.27.

A system uses 16-QAM with the sinc pulse and a binary code of rate $r=\tfrac34$. Uncoded 16-QAM needs $E_{\rm b}/N_0=13.4$ dB for $P_{\rm b}=10^{-5}$. By the bound of Proposition 6.4, how many decibels can the code gain at most?

(a) $13.4-10\log_{10}\tfrac{15}4$ dB
(b) $13.4-10\log_{10}\tfrac73$ dB
(c) $10\log_{10}\tfrac73$ dB

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

The coded system has $\eta=\tfrac34\cdot4=3$ bits per second per hertz, where Table 6.1 gives the bound $3.7$ dB. The code cannot bring the required $E_{\rm b}/N_0$ below it, so it gains at most $13.4-10\log_{10}\tfrac73=13.4-3.7=9.7$ dB, and at the same symbol rate it costs a quarter of the bit rate.

Exercise 6.28.

Two parallel channels have $|h_1|^2=|h_2|^2=1$ and $\sigma^2=1$, and each receives the energy $3$ with independent Gaussian inputs. What is the mutual information per use of the two channels?

(a) $2$ bits
(b) $\log_27$ bits
(c) $4$ bits

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

By Proposition 6.6 and equation (6.12), the rates add, $\log_2(1+3)+\log_2(1+3)=4$ bits.

Exercise 6.29.

Two parallel channels have $|h_1|^2=|h_2|^2=2$ and $\sigma^2=1$, and the total energy is $E_{\rm s}=3$. What is the largest rate over the ways of sharing the energy?

(a) $2\log_27$ bits
(b) $\log_27$ bits
(c) $4$ bits

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

With equal gains the energy is shared equally, as Section 6.4 shows, $E_1=E_2=1.5$. Equation (6.12) gives $2\log_2(1+2\cdot1.5)=2\log_24=4$ bits.

Exercise 6.30.

Two parallel channels have $|h_1|^2=1$ and $|h_2|^2=\tfrac12$, with $\sigma^2=1$ and total energy $E_{\rm s}=5$. Which split of the energy gives the largest rate?

(a) $E_1=\tfrac52$ and $E_2=\tfrac52$
(b) $E_1=3$ and $E_2=2$
(c) $E_1=5$ and $E_2=0$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

As in Problem 6.7, with $E_2=5-E_1$ the sum (6.12) is the logarithm of $(1+E_1)\bigl(1+\tfrac12(5-E_1)\bigr)=\tfrac12(1+E_1)(7-E_1)$, a parabola with zeros at $-1$ and $7$ that is largest halfway, at $E_1=3$. The best split is $E_1=3$ and $E_2=2$.

Exercise 6.31.

Two parallel channels have $|h_1|^2=1$ and $|h_2|^2=\tfrac12$, with $\sigma^2=1$, and receive $E_1=3$ and $E_2=2$. What is the rate?

(a) $\log_26$ bits
(b) $\log_212$ bits
(c) $3$ bits

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

By equation (6.12), $\log_2(1+3)+\log_2\bigl(1+\tfrac12\cdot2\bigr)=2+1=3$ bits.

Exercise 6.32.

Two parallel channels have $|h_1|^2=1$ and $|h_2|^2=\tfrac12$, with $\sigma^2=1$ and total energy $E_{\rm s}=\tfrac12$. Which split of the energy gives the largest rate?

(a) All the energy on the first channel.
(b) The same energy on each channel.
(c) All the energy on the second channel.

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

With $E_2=\tfrac12-E_1$ the product is $\tfrac12(1+E_1)(2.5-E_1)$, a parabola that is largest at $E_1=0.75$, beyond the available energy. On the interval from $0$ to $\tfrac12$ it increases, so the best choice is $E_1=\tfrac12$ and the weak channel is left unused, as at the end of Problem 6.7.

Exercise 6.33.

Why is the mutual information of several parallel channels with independent inputs the sum of their mutual informations?

(a) Because the transmitter shares the energy equally among the channels.
(b) Because all the channels have the same gain.
(c) Because the noises and the inputs are independent, so the channel law and the law of the outputs are products.

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

By the proof of Proposition 6.6, independent noises make the channel law of the outputs a product, and independent inputs make the law of the outputs a product too. The logarithm in the definition of mutual information is then a sum of one term for each channel.

Exercise 6.34.

Two parallel channels have the noise levels seen from the input $\sigma^2/|h_1|^2=1$ and $\sigma^2/|h_2|^2=4$, and the total energy is $E_{\rm s}=7$. Which split of the energy gives the largest rate?

(a) $E_1=5$ and $E_2=2$
(b) $E_1=\tfrac72$ and $E_2=\tfrac72$
(c) $E_1=7$ and $E_2=0$

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

By the remark after Problem 6.7, the best split pours the energy over the noise levels up to a common height, the rule called water-filling. The height $6$ gives $E_1=6-1=5$ and $E_2=6-4=2$, which add up to $7$, and the difference $E_1-E_2=3$ is the difference between the levels.

Exercise 6.35.

Four parallel channels have $|h_k|^2=1$ and $\sigma^2=1$, and the total energy is $E_{\rm s}=12$. What is their capacity?

(a) $\log_213$ bits
(b) $2$ bits
(c) $8$ bits

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

The gains are equal, so the energy is shared equally, $3$ for each channel, and equation (6.12) gives $4\log_2(1+3)=8$ bits per use of the four channels.

Exercise 6.36.

A link has four transmit antennas and two receive antennas. At most how many spatial modes does it have?

(a) $4$
(b) $8$
(c) $2$

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

By Section 6.5, the number of modes is the rank of the gain matrix $H$, which is at most the smaller of $n_{\rm t}$ and $n_{\rm r}$, here $2$.

Exercise 6.37.

A link with two antennas at each end has the gain matrix with rows $(3,0)$ and $(0,1)$. What are the gains $\lambda_k$ of its spatial modes?

(a) $\lambda_1=9$ and $\lambda_2=1$
(b) $\lambda_1=3$ and $\lambda_2=1$
(c) A single mode with $\lambda=10$

Show solution

The correct answer is (a) ★

Each transmit antenna reaches only one receive antenna, so the channels are already parallel, with $\tilde Y_1=3\,X_1+Z_1$ and $\tilde Y_2=X_2+Z_2$. By Proposition 6.8, $\sqrt{\lambda_1}=3$ and $\sqrt{\lambda_2}=1$, so $\lambda_1=9$ and $\lambda_2=1$.

Exercise 6.38.

A link with two antennas at each end has the gain matrix with rows $(2,1)$ and $(2,-1)$. What are the gains $\lambda_k$ of its spatial modes?

(a) A single mode with $\lambda=10$
(b) $\lambda_1=8$ and $\lambda_2=2$
(c) $\lambda_1=2$ and $\lambda_2=1$

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

The columns $(2,2)$ and $(1,-1)$ are orthogonal, with squared norms $8$ and $2$. As for $H_{\rm a}$ in Problem 6.10, sending one symbol from each antenna gives two modes, with $\lambda_1=8$ and $\lambda_2=2$. Their sum is $10$, the sum of the squared entries.

Exercise 6.39.

A link with two antennas at each end has the gain matrix with rows $(1,3)$ and $(1,3)$. What are its spatial modes?

(a) Two modes, with $\lambda_1=2$ and $\lambda_2=18$
(b) A single mode with $\lambda=10$
(c) A single mode with $\lambda=20$

Show solution

The correct answer is (c) ★

The two rows are equal, so the matrix has rank $1$ and, as for $H_{\rm b}$ in Problem 6.10, there is a single mode. Its gain is the total gain of the matrix, the sum of the squared entries, $1+9+1+9=20$.

Exercise 6.40.

A link has one transmit antenna and two receive antennas, with gains $h_1=h_2=1$, and $\mathrm{SNR}=7$. What is its capacity?

(a) $3$ bits per channel use
(b) $\log_215$ bits per channel use
(c) $6$ bits per channel use

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

By Example 6.9 there is one mode, of gain $\lambda=|h_1|^2+|h_2|^2=2$, so the capacity is $\log_2(1+2\,\mathrm{SNR})=\log_215=3.91$ bits per channel use. The second antenna doubles the SNR.

Exercise 6.41.

A link has two transmit antennas and one receive antenna, with gains $h_{11}=h_{12}=1$, and $\mathrm{SNR}=3.5$. What is its capacity?

(a) $\log_2\tfrac92$ bits per channel use
(b) $3$ bits per channel use
(c) $2\log_2\tfrac92$ bits per channel use

Show solution

The correct answer is (b) ★

The matrix is the row $(1,1)$, of rank $1$. The direction $\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,1)$ gives the gain $\lambda=2$, so by Proposition 6.8 the capacity is $\log_2(1+2\,\mathrm{SNR})=\log_28=3$ bits per channel use, which requires that the transmitter knows the gains.
